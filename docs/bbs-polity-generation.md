# BBS Polity Generation

*Status: version-3 frontier-reusing closest-fit placement, seeded CE primary-world rejection
sampling, derived detailed systems, persistent homes, contact-route visits,
six-parsec frontier materialization, and founding news implemented, 2026-08-24*

A BBS polity is intentionally a rare, favorable local stellar configuration.
It is generated by conditioning a bounded part of the ordinary Galactic
distribution, not by searching progressively larger spheres around Earth and
not by changing the global stellar-density model.

Candidate locations are selected relative to the current plotted frontier.
Once accepted, all systems, seeds, and resolved empty volume are persisted in
the same form as any other materialized region. The resulting polity does not
retain a special ability to reroll its neighborhood.

Adding a BBS can extend the historical settlement envelope used when later
systems are materialized. It does not rewrite populations in systems that
already exist. See
[`settlement-and-system-survey.md`](settlement-and-system-survey.md).

## Implemented closest-fit search

Version 3 treats the BBS capital as the canonical position used to compare
candidate sites. It constructs candidates from every existing system in 98
canonical primitive directions and four rotations around each direction. It
considers unvisited, unaligned, inhabited frontier systems in Sol's Jump-2
component as prospective inward polity members. Existing systems retain their
exact coordinates; they are never snapped to the quarter-parsec coverage
lattice. New positions receive bounded deterministic three-dimensional
variation and are then quantized to that lattice before the ordinary invariant
checks. The capital and its two J-1 companions vary as one group so their
relative trade-route geometry is preserved.

Both the reused-member and version-2 external-anchor forms have outward and
lateral families. The external lateral form is essential: without it, the
form normally selected in generated universes projected every new cluster
away from the materialized volume. Sixteen deterministic geometry variants
are available as ordered fallbacks, but the search stops at the first variant
with an eligible unresolved candidate rather than paying for all variants on
every configuration. A three-parsec spatial index limits each invariant check
to systems capable of participating in a boundary crossing.

The search rejects candidates whose new positions touch resolved cells or
which violate any invariant below. Within the first viable geometry variant,
candidates are ordered by:

1. capital distance to the nearest system belonging to an existing polity;
2. local conditioning cost; and
3. a placement-seed-derived tie key.

Only the winning candidate is written. Rejected candidate checks are
read-only and do not mark space surveyed. If no candidate is eligible, the
sysop receives a normal `noEligibleSite` rule rejection and configuration is
not partially committed.

Version 3 retains the ten-system invariant topology but adopts the candidate's
existing frontier anchor as its inward member instead of placing all ten
systems beyond that anchor. The anchor's immutable generation seed, celestial
details, and names are preserved; only its polity affiliation changes. When a
reuse candidate wins, the other nine members are generated in unresolved
space. Outward and lateral templates are considered for both reused and
external anchors, so closest-fit ranking can place a polity beside the plotted
volume instead of forcing every polity to project radially outward. Version 2
placed all ten members beyond an external anchor and considered only the
outward form.

Version 3 also creates one unaligned outward gateway system inside the
resolved guard. The six-parsec survey supplies a larger plotted shell beyond
that guard. A later polity can anchor on the far side of this existing frontier
and use a route of already-real systems back through it. This lets inhabited
space expand without inserting a new star into previously surveyed empty
space.

Before testing candidates, versions 2 and 3 find the complete graph of plotted
systems reachable from Sol by successive Jump-2 legs. At least one inward
gateway endpoint must belong to that graph. Since every accepted cluster is
internally Jump-2-connected, its capital and every member then have a plotted
Jump-2 path to Sol. Founding marks a deterministic shortest path from the
candidate's anchor to already visited space as the contact ship's actual
route to Sol. That narrow bridge becomes visited and traffic-active; adjacent plotted
systems remain frontier. A gateway into one of the disconnected components of
the initial nearby-star catalogue is not sufficient by itself. Persisted
version-1 clusters are historical universe state and are not moved or
regenerated by this rule.

## Galactic eligibility

This defensible cluster topology is permitted only in the Galactic annulus:

```text
6,000 pc <= Rgc <= 11,000 pc
```

where:

```text
Rgc = hypot(8,178 pc - coreward_x, spinward_y)
```

The entire cluster and its resolved three-parsec guard volume must lie inside
the annulus, not merely its capital or nominal center. On the direct radial
axis from Earth, this permits about 2,178 pc of coreward placement and about
2,822 pc of rimward placement.

The coreward boundary keeps this special topology outside the Galactic
bar/bulge region, where the version-1 repeated-arm exponential disk is not a
credible model and the required sparse boundary becomes implausible. The
rimward boundary is the first major outer-disk density-break region; beyond
it, manufacturing a compact polity cluster becomes progressively less
credible and the smooth exponential disk also becomes an incomplete model.

The annulus is necessary but not sufficient. Versions 1 and 2 accept capital
locations with an unconditioned background intensity from `0.020` through
`0.300` stellar components per cubic parsec. Their secondary conditioning
cost is the absolute logarithmic ratio between that intensity and the Solar
neighborhood intensity. This accounts for spiral-arm enhancement, inter-arm
space, and Galactic north/south displacement. The bounds and cost are
versioned generation rules, not universal astronomical constants.

This restriction applies only to provisioning this particular BBS-polity
shape. Ordinary systems, travel, and later inner-bar, bulge, or outer-disk
civilization models may exist outside the annulus.

## Cluster topology

Let `C` be the set of systems assigned to the new BBS polity. The initial
target is roughly ten systems, subject to later gameplay and generation
testing. The systems in `C` must form a useful internal Jump network rather
than an arbitrary concentration of stars.

The boundary has the following hard constraints:

1. There are **one to three gateway crossings** between a system in `C` and a
   system outside `C`.
2. Every gateway crossing is within Jump-2 range: its endpoints are no more
   than two parsecs apart. A gateway may instead be within Jump-1 range.
3. Every other pair consisting of one system in `C` and one system outside
   `C` is **more than three parsecs apart**. Consequently, direct entry anywhere
   except a gateway requires Jump-4 or better.
4. Gateway count is the number of boundary-crossing system pairs, not merely
   the number of outside systems. A second short edge to the same outside
   system is a second defensible approach and counts as another gateway.

The cluster must be internally connected for the normal starting fleet.
Exact internal diameter, redundancy, and gateway placement remain balance
parameters, but the conditioned generator should reject brittle accidental
chains unless a particular polity design explicitly calls for one.

At least one gateway must join the existing system-to-system Jump-2 component
that contains Sol. This is a whole-route constraint, not merely a requirement
that some already materialized system lie within range.

This boundary channels normal traffic and gives pickets, patrols, customs, and
blockades meaningful places to operate. It is not an absolute force field.
The established empty-space Jump rules still allow a suitably fueled ship to
stage through deep space, accepting additional time, fuel, navigation risk,
and failure exposure. A direct non-gateway system-to-system entry nevertheless
requires J-4 or better.

## Capital and subsistence routes

The BBS home world is the polity's TL12 capital and remains the highest-
technology world in the starting polity. It must have **two different
inhabited systems within Jump-1 range**.

Those neighboring systems must have complementary economic profiles that
support ordinary two-way trade with the capital. The current generator
requires one to carry the core CE Agricultural trade code and the other the
Industrial trade code. They are not guaranteed jackpot routes, but each must
support a viable subsistence loop for a modest Jump-1 trader after normal
operating costs. Two neighbors provide both route choice and resilience if
conditions or local events temporarily impair one market.

The capital and both complementary J-1 systems have an additional operational
guard. A prospective seed must derive a safe primary-world Jump locus at no
more than **3.5 game days** of constant-1G, midpoint-turnover travel. The guard
evaluates epoch zero and the periapsis, quadrature, and apoapsis phases of the
primary-world orbit and every companion-star orbit. The same authoritative
geometry includes the actual positions and 100-diameter exclusions of all
stars and bodies, plus the universal half-day minimum safety clearance.
Failure rejects only that prospective system seed.

## Materialization boundary

The polity transaction first resolves enough surrounding volume to prove the
boundary constraints permanently: the union of the three-parsec neighborhoods
around every cluster system. Only after that protected core is fixed does it
materialize the still-unresolved portions of the six-parsec arrival footprint
around every member. The transaction then:

1. generates or reads all stellar components in that guard volume;
2. assigns the accepted cluster membership and gateway crossings;
3. verifies that no uncounted outside system lies within three parsecs of any
   cluster system;
4. marks every polity member and every plotted system crossed by the founding
   contact route as visited, leaving the unaligned stub and remaining generated
   shell unvisited; and
5. persists seeded system records, surveyed empty cells, polity membership,
   and the BBS-home record atomically. Immutable celestial details remain
   derived rather than becoming database rows.

Later frontier materialization therefore cannot insert an accidental J-1,
J-2, or J-3 entrance into the protected boundary. The outer three-to-six-
parsec shell may contain J-2 paths around that core and can support later
far-side expansion, but it carries no generated traffic while unvisited.

The founding event originates one headline agency-news packet at the polity
capital. Its dossier lists every registered polity system. Receipt of that
single public packet merges the complete polity into a captain's Known
Universe; it does not disclose the unaligned stub or surrounding frontier.

## Conditional generation

The authoritative engine obtains one 256-bit placement seed from the operating
system CSPRNG. That seed initializes an HMAC-SHA-256 counter stream. The
materializer draws prospective 256-bit system seeds from the stream, runs the
core CE primary-world generator, derives version-1 celestial geometry for the
three core systems, and rejects draws until it obtains:

- one inhabited TL12 capital;
- one inhabited Agricultural J-1 companion no more advanced than TL12;
- one inhabited Industrial J-1 companion no more advanced than TL12;
- seven other inhabited polity members no more advanced than TL12; and
- one unconstrained, unaligned outward gateway system.

Each role has a bounded 65,536-draw safety limit. Accepted system seeds and the
placement seed are persisted; rejected draws do not become systems or resolved
universe state. Regenerating the accepted primary world from its system seed
reproduces the authoritative UWP fields exactly.

The implemented CE generator covers primary-world size, atmosphere,
hydrographics, population and multiplier, starport, government, Law Level,
Tech Level and minimums, planetoid-belt count, gas-giant count, and derived
Agricultural/Industrial predicates. Core CE does not provide the full random
star, orbit, and planetary-system generator needed here.

That missing source has now been identified: *Unmerciful Frontier: The CCA
Sourcebook*, third edition, pages 44–149, contains the Clement Sector detailed
system generator and explicitly designates that section's generation method as
Open Game Content. Its useful rules cover stellar object and spectral type,
multiple stars and separation, stellar zones and limits, gas giants, belts,
rocky worlds, orbital placement and eccentricity, moons, periods, rotation,
temperature, atmosphere, and hydrographics. The implementation adapts those
mechanics rather than their setting:

- the implemented continuous Milky Way density replaces hex occupancy;
- standard CE Jump drive replaces Zimm Points and every Zimm-specific step;
- core CE remains authoritative for overlapping UWP, belt, gas-giant,
  starport, and trade-code procedures; and
- Clement refinements use named, versioned feature streams so they do not
  change an existing primary world.

That domain-separated expansion is implemented in
[`celestial-system-generation.md`](celestial-system-generation.md). The
accepted system seed is its complete immutable source.

Conditioning remains local and does not modify the density function used for
unrelated frontier materialization.
