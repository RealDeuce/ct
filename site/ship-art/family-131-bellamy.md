# Bellamy Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 131*

## Mechanical identity

- Members: `ship-131` Bellamy, `ship-132` Bellamy Plus, and `ship-133`
  Bellamy Grav
- Shipyard path: Path 1, Concord Exchange Yards
- Hull: 400 displacement tons / approximately 5,600 m³
- Dimensions: 60 m long, 21 m wide, and 14 m high
- Configuration: standard; technology level 11
- Protection: one armor point, radiation shielding, and self-sealing hull
- Drives: Jump-D / Jump-2, maneuver B / thrust 1, and power D
- Endurance: one Jump and three weeks of power-plant fuel
- Working fit: ten staterooms, eight middle passengers, 172 tons cargo, two
  fuel processors, workshop, medical bed, gymnasium, and recreation space
- Defense: two double beam-laser turrets, one mixed double missile/sandcaster
  turret, 12 missiles, 20 sandcaster canisters, and one reserved hardpoint

The three source-era names describe production and gravitic refit history, but
normalization to Cepheus Engine machinery makes their published construction
records mechanically identical. No legal exterior difference remains: the
same family plate is therefore used for all three dossiers. Inventing cosmetic
variants would falsely imply different drives, volume, protection, or role.

## Canonical architecture

The Bellamy occupies a 60 × 21 × 14 m envelope and uses three balanced volumes:
a compact faceted command prow, a broad rectangular cargo/passenger body with a
shallow upper occupied deck, and a neatly faired square engineering block.
Clipped corners, steps, and modular joints keep it a standard space hull rather
than an atmospheric body or a derivative of another 400-ton family.

A low protected bridge brow faces forward. Five small dark-bronze cabin windows
on the visible port deck represent approximately half of the ten staterooms.
One pressure airlock precedes exactly two equal broad ribbed freight shutters;
their rectangular chrome collars and simple loading route make the 172-ton hold
legible. A continuous layered seam band marks radiation shielding, while the
light edge band communicates only one armor point.

One segmented non-glowing Jump-service radiator and two low rectangular field-
coil service housings identify Jump-D. Two modest maneuver apertures identify
the slow B drive; one power grille and exactly two processor grilles complete
the machinery map. The record has no scoop, so neither an intake nor a scoop
grid may appear.

## Weapon map

Four hardpoint coordinates exist. The standard port three-quarter view exposes
two installed mounts: a forward dorsal double-beam cup with two narrow optical
apertures and a lower-port mixed cup with one closed missile shutter and one
blunt sandcaster projector. The second double-beam mount and the closed unused
hardpoint are hidden on the far/ventral side. Thus two of three fitted turrets
are visible without pretending the reserved station is armed. There is no point
defense.

## Concord finish

- Command, central pressure body, and cargo shutters: warm ivory `#F1E3C2`
- Lower hull, recessed structure, and engineering block: royal blue `#2354A3`
- Airlock, shutter corner marks, and service panels: vermilion `#E44B2D`
- Cargo collars, datum rails, turret rings, shield band, field housings, and
  drive surrounds: polished aluminum/chrome
- Glazing: dark bronze
- Character: bright maintained scheduled-route equipment with restrained wear
  at the cargo sills, never camouflage
