# Silk Road Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 182*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-182`; `ship-182` Silk Road
- Hull: 4,500 displacement tons / approximately 63,000 m³
- Dimensions: 150 m long, 60 m wide, and 28 m high
- Configuration: standard; technology level 11
- Protection: four armor points from one crystaliron layer
- Drives: Jump-Z / Jump-2, maneuver Z, and power Z / thrust 2
- Endurance: one Jump and two weeks of power-plant fuel
- Control: basic civilian electronics, holographic controls, capital-core/3
  computer, emergency power, and twenty-five unused fire-control stations
- Personnel/support: 79 crew, three-bed medical bay, three workshops, offices,
  detention spaces, repair drones, and 2,647.2 tons of cargo
- Boat facilities: one 60-ton full hangar carrying two Wayfarer Cargo boats
  (`ship-181`)
- Armament: twenty double-beam turrets and forty-five point-defense lasers
- Reserved battery: twenty-five empty hardpoints
- Ammunition: none
- Fuel handling: no scoop and no processor

## Canonical commercial carrier

Silk Road occupies a 150 × 60 × 28 m standard pressure hull: a long modular
rectangular body, blunt faceted command prow, six enormous boxlike freight
cells, repeated transverse load frames, strong lower keel, narrow service spine,
and massive square engineering block. Projecting access and handling components
reinforce the non-aerodynamic configuration. It may enter atmosphere badly but
must never become a smooth wedge or lifting body.

One crystaliron layer protects working structure without producing dreadnought
armor terraces. The broad civilian bridge has exactly ten dark panes. Paired
flush cheek strips carry basic commercial sensors. Six service doors, cargo
rails, tug sockets, fold-flat grapples, handholds, repair-drone slots, and
emergency-power access make port handling legible at human scale.

## Freight and boat architecture

Six huge closed ribbed freight shutters form two rows of three along the port
side. Each approximately 23 × 9 m door remains separately framed, closed, and
unarmed. Together they dominate the silhouette and make the 2,647.2-ton cargo
role credible. Door frames and handling rails may be bright, but no shutter is
a missile bank, hangar, or broadside.

One separate closed approximately 24 × 10 m aft-port-upper full-hangar shutter
has at least 23 m clear internal axis and sufficient beam for two canonical
18.8 m Wayfarer Cargo boats. Both 30-ton craft remain inside and do not appear
beside the carrier.

## Installed and reserved hardpoint map

The 4,500-ton hull has forty-five hardpoints. Twenty carry double-beam turrets;
twenty-five remain empty. The standard view divides the inventory as follows:

| Station state | Visible | Hidden | Total |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Active double-beam turret | 10 | 10 | 20 |
| Capped spare hardpoint | 12 | 13 | 25 |
| All hardpoints | 22 | 23 | 45 |

Each active station uses one common 3–4 m ring and a compact cup with exactly
two narrow optical emitters. The ten visible cups occupy forward dorsal,
forward port shoulder, forward port flank, mid dorsal, mid upper port flank,
mid lower port flank, aft dorsal, aft port shoulder, port ventral lip, and
stern-upper port quarter.

The twelve visible spare rings remain smooth chrome-rimmed armored disks among
the remaining dorsal, shoulder, flank, ventral-lip, forward, and aft positions.
They carry no raised cup, barrel, lens, or shutter. Hidden active and spare
stations cover the starboard shoulders, deeper ventral surface, and far
forward/aft quarters.

This distribution gives the installed battery useful overlapping coverage now
and prevents future refits from inheriting a crown-only station map. Preserve
both active and capped coordinates. Do not arm all visible disks merely because
the hull has space, and do not add a weapon bay, barbette, missile launcher,
sandcaster, or railgun.

## Point-defense map

Forty-five point-defense nodes split twenty-two visible and twenty-three hidden.
The visible tiny blue-white lenses form five groups: five at the prow, five on
forward dorsal/shoulder armor, four on the mid port flank, four on the aft
dorsal/shoulder, and four along the port ventral lip.

Each 0.4–0.8 m lens is much smaller than either an active turret or capped
hardpoint. PD optics remain distributed and barrel-free, never decorative roof
guns or launch cells.

## Drives and fuel recognition

Jump-Z uses two long parallel non-glowing segmented radiator bands and four
paired rectangular field-coil/service housings on the upper-aft spine. Those
boxlike structures are machinery and remain barrel-free. Six large recessed
maneuver apertures and four ribbed power grilles identify the Z/Z two-gravity
stern without borrowing Trafalgar's aggressively faired battle geometry.

Silk Road has neither a fuel scoop nor processors. Do not add an intake,
processing grille, external tank, or generic glowing Jump ring.

## Concord finish

Concord Exchange Yards uses warm ivory `#F1E3C2`, royal blue `#2354A3`,
vermilion `#E44B2D`, and polished aluminum/chrome. Ivory covers command and
freight surfaces; blue marks keel, engineering, and working bands; vermilion
defines cargo, hangar, airlock, and safety thresholds; chrome protects freight
collars, service interfaces, hardpoint rims, and drive fittings. The effect is
prosperous high-volume logistics, legible and maintained rather than naval.
