# Charon Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 005*

## Mechanical identity

- Family: `family-5`, Charon
- Members: `ship-5` Charon and `ship-158` Charon
- Hull: 10 displacement tons / approximately 140 m³
- Configuration: streamlined
- Technology level: 11
- Shared drives: `sC` maneuver and `sC` power / thrust 6
- Shared control: one-person cockpit
- Shared accommodation: six acceleration seats
- Shared protection: two points of whole-hull crystaliron armor
- Shared access: one pressure airlock
- Shared endurance: one week
- Shared limitations: no Jump drive, no installed weapon, and only 0.35 tons
  of cargo

The two records are mechanically exterior-equivalent. They are separate
catalog records because their source and mission descriptions differ, not
because they represent different production fits. One approved exterior plate
must therefore be reused for both dossiers.

## Canonical family anchor

The Charon is a fast single-deck passenger launch built as a compact armored
lifting body rather than a miniature airliner. Its canonical maximum dimensions
are 12.5 m long, 5.2 m wide, and 3.8 m high. The smoothly tapered pressure body,
shallow underside, and short shoulder chines occupy approximately 55 percent of
that bounding envelope, plausibly enclosing the required approximately 140 m³.

The silhouette is a blunt faceted cockpit nose flowing into a long horizontal
six-seat passenger band and a broad, neatly faired aft maneuver section. A
human-scale rectangular pressure airlock sits on the visible port side just aft
of the passenger band. Three small, evenly spaced passenger viewports are
visible on the port side; they imply the paired six-seat cabin without creating
a glass wall. A narrow flush cargo locker is below the aft cabin.

The `sC` installation reads as two large recessed circular field housings at the
aft quarters plus a smaller central power-service aperture. Armor is shown as a
continuous cream-colored crystaliron shell with deep-set glazing, protected
seams, flush sensors, and no fragile projections. The unused hardpoint is a
single closed circular service plate on the dorsal centerline, not a turret.

### Invariants

Both catalog records preserve exactly:

- the 12.5 × 5.2 × 3.8 m envelope and single-deck arrangement;
- cockpit, three-port-window passenger band, airlock, cargo locker, and closed
  dorsal hardpoint positions;
- aft field-housing and power-service geometry;
- hull seams, landing/docking access, camera, crop, perspective, and lighting;
- Concord Exchange construction detail and livery; and
- the absence of weapons, Jump-drive cues, external missiles, wings, tail,
  landing skids, and decorative spacecraft.

## Concord Exchange finish

- Primary shell: warm ivory `#F1E3C2`
- Passenger/airlock identification: vermilion `#E44B2D`
- Lower chine and aft service panels: royal blue `#2354A3`
- Trim: polished aluminum and bright chrome around the airlock, field housings,
  viewport band, and service seams
- Condition: prosperous scheduled transport, clean and maintained, with only
  light atmospheric wear along the underside and airlock threshold

The same finish serves both records. Do not add medical symbols to `ship-158`:
medical transfer is a secondary use of the standard launch, not a fitted
ambulance conversion.

## Catalog plate

- Canvas: 3:2 landscape
- View: complete front-port three-quarter, 12 degrees above centerline
- Perspective: restrained near-orthographic
- Lighting: warm upper front-port key, weak cool aft rim
- Backdrop: charcoal-black sparse starfield and faint teal plotting grid
- Medium: original 1970s/1980s gouache-and-airbrush technical catalog plate
- Prohibited: text, labels, logos, watermarks, other craft, weapon fire, motion
  blur, planets, dramatic lens distortion, modern military-gray default, or
  recognizable franchise design
