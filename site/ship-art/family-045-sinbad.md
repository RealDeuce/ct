# Sinbad Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 045*

## Mechanical identity

- Family: `family-45`, Sinbad
- Members: `ship-45` and `ship-46`
- Shipyard path: Path 2, Venture Passage Works
- Hull: 200 displacement tons / approximately 2,800 m³
- Configuration: streamlined; technology level 11
- Shared protection: four armor points
- Shared performance: Jump drive B / Jump-2, maneuver and power drive C /
  thrust 3, one jump, and two weeks of power endurance
- Shared accommodation: eleven staterooms, ten low berths, one-bed medical bay,
  and a six-person operating crew
- Shared armament: one triple turret with beam laser, missile rack, and
  sandcaster; twelve missiles and twenty sand canisters; one second hardpoint
  remains closed and empty
- Shared utility: fuel processing, approximately 4.5-ton air/raft bay, and a
  41-42-ton freight hold

Ship 45 uses basic civilian electronics, a Model/2bis computer, a generic
4.5-ton hangar record, and 42 tons cargo. Ship 46 uses basic military
electronics, a Model/3 computer, an air/raft hangar label, and 41 tons cargo.
These are internal equipment and allocation differences within one free-trader
fit. Exact exterior reuse is required.

## Canonical family architecture

The Sinbad is 43.0 m long, 15.5 m wide, and 10.5 m high with three occupied
levels. It is a forward-driving clipped lozenge wrapped around a tall central
pressure spine: a faceted command prow, two thick swept shoulder rails
protecting the passenger waist, and a broad armored aft maneuver block. The
tapered envelope plausibly encloses approximately 2,800 m³. It must look more
protected and thrust-oriented than the broad commercial Polo without becoming
a naval warship.

The command prow has an eight-port bridge brow and one recessed port personnel
hatch. Twelve small dark windows form one long protected occupancy rhythm along
the upper port waist: eleven stateroom windows and one medical/common window.
Below them, five paired low-berth service panels account for the ten berths
without becoming passenger windows. One
closed 8.5 × 4.0 m two-leaf cargo shutter occupies the lower midbody. A separate
closed 5.0 × 3.0 m air/raft hangar door lies aft, with a heavy chrome frame and
no visible vehicle.

One common 3.5 m dorsal triple-turret ring carries three visibly different
apertures: one narrow beam-laser optic, one closed rectangular missile shutter,
and one blunt three-port sandcaster projector. A second circular hardpoint cover
lies flush aft and remains closed. All ammunition stays internal.

The internal Jump drive reads through two low rectangular coil-service cheeks
embedded symmetrically in the shoulder rails and one transverse segmented
heat-rejection band ahead of engineering. These are armored access landmarks,
not a glowing ring. Two large hexagonal maneuver apertures, one central
heat-stained power grille, one flush ventral fuel-scoop grid, six landing-pad
doors, paired docking guides, protected chrome edge rails, and strong shoulder
seams complete the chassis.
