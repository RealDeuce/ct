# Magellan Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 067*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-67`; `ship-67` Magellan
- Shipyard path: Path 4, Civic Survey Works
- Hull: 400 displacement tons / approximately 5,600 m³
- Dimensions: 62 m long, 22 m wide, and 15.5 m high
- Configuration: streamlined; technology level 11; non-standard design
- Protection: four whole points of crystaliron armor
- Drives: Jump-D / Jump-2, one Jump; maneuver H and power H / thrust 4
- Endurance: two weeks of power-plant fuel
- Control and complement: standard bridge, basic military electronics,
  Model/3 computer, and seven crew
- Accommodation: six staterooms, four low berths, one emergency low berth,
  and a one-bed medical bay
- Expedition support: ten fuel processors, four probe drones, repair drones,
  a closed unassigned fourteen-ton standard hangar, and 127 tons cargo
- Armament: two double-beam turrets, one double-sandcaster turret, and one
  double-missile turret; 24 standard missiles and 40 sand canisters

Magellan is a self-reliant exploration scout and rescue ship. Its processor
capacity, probes, accommodation, utility bay, substantial hold, and four-
gravity machinery support long independent expeditions. Its four double cups
provide broad defensive coverage without turning it into a frontline warship.

## Independent exploration-caravel chassis

The 62 × 22 × 15.5 m streamlined hull is an “exploration caravel”: tall blunt
faceted command forecastle, long terraced occupied expedition body, thick
integral sensor shoulders, deep continuous sample-and-cargo keel, raised aft
mission/observation quarterdeck, narrowed machinery waist, and broad thrust-
four stern. All volumes belong to one pressure hull rather than wings, pods,
tanks, or a distributed frame. The tapered and terraced envelope plausibly
contains approximately 5,600 m³.

Magellan is independent of other Civic scouts. Never enlarge Darwin's low
survey-lantern chassis or its two laboratory windows; never copy Challenger's
survey compass, Janus's split mask, or Warden's customs shield. It is also
independent of Argosy's Venture cargo whale and Verne's packet-liner family.

Eight protected panes cross the command-forecastle brow. Four of six
stateroom windows are visible along the terraced port occupancy band and two
are hidden starboard, an intentional corridor asymmetry. A single broad dark-
teal mission-observation bay in a chrome faceted frame occupies the raised aft
quarterdeck. It is an instrument workspace, not a second bridge, hangar, cargo
door, or weapon aperture.

Two circular instrument collars, two ceramic radomes, one fine antenna grid,
and small optical flats serve exploration and basic-military sensing. One
closed formal docking/airlock hatch, two visible/two hidden low-berth panels,
one plain emergency-berth panel, one plain medical-service hatch, and one
repair-drone slot complete the occupied section. No service panel carries a
cross, badge, text, or emblem.

## Cargo, utility hangar, probes, and fuel

One enormous closed 14 × 5 m four-leaf cargo/sample shutter occupies the lower
port keel. Three vertical divisions sit inside one continuous reinforced frame,
with handling rails and a low sill. It serves the 127-ton hold; never open it
or show cargo, equipment, or a vehicle inside.

One separate smaller closed 6 × 4.2 m two-leaf utility-hangar shutter occupies
the aft port shoulder, with at least a 13 m internal axis and clean recovery
guides. The standard hangar contains fourteen tons of capacity but no catalogued
craft. Keep it closed, empty, and unassigned: no launch, vehicle, drone, clamp,
or invented carried craft may appear.

Exactly four probe drones are represented by four small closed launch/service
coffers: two visible port and two hidden starboard. The coffers remain far
smaller than turret rings and the hangar. No probe is deployed in the plate.

Ten fuel processors form a bilateral bank. Five round ribbed grilles appear in
a tidy row above the port cargo shutter; five matching units are hidden
starboard. One adjacent manifold and circular coupling serve refueling. There
is no recorded scoop, so no belly intake or field-grid region may be added.

## Weapon and coverage map

Exactly four hardpoint coordinates provide complementary hemispheres:

1. visible forward upper-port shoulder: double beam-laser turret with exactly
   two matching short optical emitters;
2. visible lower-aft port flank/keel lip: double-sandcaster turret with exactly
   two matching blunt multi-port projectors;
3. hidden upper-aft starboard shoulder: the second double-beam turret; and
4. hidden lower-forward starboard/ventral station: double-missile turret.

The front-port plate therefore exposes exactly two of four turrets, close to
the physically visible half of the hull. The hidden weapons do not migrate to
the crown for display. Forward/aft, upper/lower, and port/starboard allocation
gives useful overlapping arcs and protects the drive, cargo, and observation
approaches. All missiles and sand canisters remain internal.

No point-defense nodes are installed. Never arm an instrument collar, radome,
antenna, probe coffer, observation bay, processor, coupling, door latch,
recovery eye, or closed landing pad.

## Jump, drive, and armor landmarks

The internal Jump-D drive reads through two segmented transverse radiator/
service bands around the aft machinery waist and four oval coil-service
blisters, two visible and two hidden. There is no exterior Jump ring, luminous
portal, or glow.

Three broad maneuver apertures are visible in a vertical port stern bank and
three matching starboard apertures are hidden. Two smaller separate power
grilles serve the H/H thrust-four machinery. Four armor points read through two
flowing protective bands around forecastle, shoulders, cargo keel and mission
deck, reinforced aperture frames, and overlapping serviceable plates.
