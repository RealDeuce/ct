# Bacon Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 207*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-207`; `ship-207` Bacon
- Shipyard path: Path 4, Civic Survey Works
- Hull: 200 displacement tons / approximately 2,800 m³
- Dimensions: 50 m long, 18 m wide, and 12.5 m high
- Configuration: standard; technology level 9; standard design
- Protection: two titanium-steel armor points
- Drives: Jump A / Jump-1; maneuver A and power A / thrust 1
- Endurance: one Jump-1 and four weeks of power-plant fuel
- Mission support: nine crew, six staterooms, three emergency low berths, six
  laboratories, three probe-drone systems, two processors, scoop, and 29 tons
  expedition cargo
- Boat facilities: two lifeboat hangars, each carrying one Dory (`ship-194`)
- Armament: none; both hardpoints/fire-control stations unused

Bacon is an independent research vessel, not a passenger liner or armed survey
cutter. Its exterior privileges laboratory access, probe operations, sample
handling, and two redundant launches. Modest armor and a slow drive support
field reliability; they do not turn the ship into a military citadel.

## Canonical instrument barge

The 50 × 18 × 12.5 m standard hull is a non-streamlined tiered instrument
barge: short faceted command prow, deep rectangular pressure body, raised
offset laboratory terrace, integrated port launch cells, expedition belly,
and compact square engineering stern. One light protected edge band expresses
the two-point armor fit.

A seven-pane bridge brow and three protected upper observation/cabin panes
provide occupied scale. Three large visible instrument modules represent the
port half of six laboratories; each combines a dark-teal optical flat, ribbed
heat exchanger, and lime sample-service drawers. Their hidden partners occupy
the far side. Optical flats are barrel-free scientific instruments, not guns
or panoramic passenger windows.

Exactly three small lime shutters on the laboratory terrace serve the three
probe-drone systems. They remain closed and no drone is visible. One smaller
aft cyan shutter serves the separate 29-ton expedition/sample hold; it must not
be confused with either launch cell. Emergency-berth access, specimen-transfer
collar, antenna flats, handholds, and clean maintenance panels remain human-
scale and unarmed. The mast-and-dome assembly is a scientific sensor cluster,
never a turret.

## Dory launch cells and empty hardpoints

Two equal closed cyan shutters in the lower port side each serve one lifeboat
hangar. Each has approximately 8.5 × 5.5 m frontage and at least a 16 m
internal axis for one canonical 14.5 × 7.5 × 4.5 m Dory. Both launches stay
inside and invisible. The two hangars are separate installed components, not
one giant bay, external clamps, or an armed flight deck.

The vessel has two unused hardpoints. One forward upper-port/shoulder position
appears as a smooth flush circular cover; its ventral/starboard partner is
hidden. Never add a turret housing, emitter, launcher, PD node, decorative
barrel, or second visible cover merely to illustrate the inventory.

## Drive, fuel, and Civic Survey recognition

Jump A / Jump-1 uses one compact non-glowing upper-aft radiator strip and two
small coil/service blisters. Two modest stern field apertures and one power
grille identify the one-gravity A/A plant. One flush lower port/ventral scoop
grid and two processor grilles support field refueling. Never enlarge the
drive into a fast-ship stern or add an external tank, flame, or glowing ring.

Civic Survey Works uses warm white `#F4F0D8`, saturated cyan `#25A9B8`, lime
`#A8C83E`, polished chrome/aluminum, and dark teal recesses. White organizes
the pressure hull and lab casings; cyan identifies laboratory, launch, sensor,
and expedition-service boundaries; lime identifies probes, specimens, safety,
and processors; chrome protects optical, hardpoint, Jump, scoop, and drive
collars. Bright public-service color makes the ship legible and maintained,
not sterile, militarized, or gray.
