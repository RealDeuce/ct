# Aquarius Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production singleton family 084*

## Mechanical identity

- Record: `ship-84` Aquarius
- Path: Admiralty Line Works (P7)
- Hull: 1,000 displacement tons / approximately 14,000 m³; distributed
- Dimensions: 84.0 m long, 36.0 m wide, and 22.0 m high overall
- Fit: TL11, no armor, Jump H / Jump-2, maneuver and power H / thrust 2, two
  weeks endurance, and advanced electronics
- Tanker support: five staterooms, emergency low berth, repair drones, 607 tons
  of fuel/store capacity, folded transfer booms, and formal manifolds
- Boat: one Argo in a 20-ton standard hangar
- Weapons: two single-beam turrets, two single-missile turrets, two double-
  sandcaster turrets, and four unused fire-control stations; no point defense
- Fuel limitation: no scoop or processor bank

Aquarius is an independent fleet-tanker family. It must not reuse the Klondike
module tender, Ithaca modular freighter, or another distributed chassis. The
formal axial order, repeated frames, component collars, and livery establish
Admiralty manufacture.

## Canonical architecture

The hull is an 84.0 × 36.0 × 22.0 m “axial replenishment train”: a compact
faceted five-pane command-and-boat drum at the prow, heavy exposed central
service spine with real open voids, four enormous enclosed rounded-octagonal
tank/store drums in port/starboard pairs, smaller manifold waist, and broad
separate aft engineering battery. Repeated transverse frames and bright-metal
datum rails align every permanent pressure volume.

The four drums dominate the ship because the 607-ton tanker hold dominates its
mission. Each drum has three broad reinforcement bands, one closed inspection
shutter, one bright transfer coupling, and protected manifold connections to
the spine. The drums belong to the distributed hull; they are not detachable
external tanks, modules, docking loads, or carried craft. Two articulated
transfer booms stow tightly along the port spine beside hose reels and docking
guides. They are industrial equipment, not weapons.

One closed 8.0 × 5.4 m ribbed two-leaf standard-hangar shutter occupies the
visible port side of the command-and-boat drum. Its approximately 18 m internal
axis clears the approved 16.5 × 7.0 × 4.8 m Argo. The launch remains internal.
Exactly three of five stateroom windows appear on the port command drum and two
are hidden starboard. One personnel airlock, plain emergency-berth panel,
repair-drone slot, and service access remain closed and unmarked.

## Ten-coordinate coverage map

Ten hardpoint coordinates divide evenly between visible and occluded halves.
Five visible coordinates contain three weapons and two blank covers:

1. Forward upper-port command shoulder: one single beam-laser optic.
2. Upper-mid port spine/outrigger beside the first tank pair: one closed single
   missile-rack shutter.
3. Lower-aft port spine beside the second tank pair: one double-sandcaster cup
   with exactly two blunt multi-port projectors.
4. Dorsal-forward spine: closed unused hardpoint cover.
5. Lower-mid port keel/spine: closed unused hardpoint cover.

The hidden half repeats the equipment totals with one single beam turret, one
single missile turret, one double sandcaster turret, and two unused covers
across starboard and ventral arcs. The standard plate therefore shows roughly
half the ten coordinates and half the six installed weapons without building a
dorsal gun row. Hidden stations never migrate to the visible crown.

There are no point-defense nodes. Tank couplings, boom pivots, hose reels,
manifolds, inspection shutters, hangar fittings, frame joints, docking guides,
sensors, and service latches remain visibly industrial and unarmed.

## Jump, drive, and structural treatment

The internal Jump-H drive reads through two ribbed aft field-vane service
housings and one segmented transverse radiator/service band on the engineering
battery. There is no exterior Jump ring, hoop, portal, glow, or tank. Two large
port maneuver apertures and two hidden starboard apertures flank two separate
power grilles at the thrust-two aft battery.

Aquarius has no armor. Pressure-vessel skins, frames, and opening surrounds are
serviceable and robust, but never become layered battleship plating. The ship
also has no scoop or processor bank: it transfers carried refined fuel and
stores rather than harvesting or refining wilderness fuel.

## Admiralty finish and catalog plate

- Deep navy `#203B69`: axial spine, structural frames, command and engineering
  lower fields
- Warm white `#E8E5D4`: all four tank/store drums, command upper panels,
  hangar shutter, and selected engineering faces
- Signal red `#C43C35`: transfer couplings, boom safety bands, recovery marks,
  hardpoint collars, and selected radiator/drive panels
- Bright aluminum: datum rails, tank reinforcement bands, turret cups, opening
  frames, manifolds, and service interfaces
- Gunmetal/dark blue: recesses, grilles, pipes, shutters, glazing, and apertures

Color is formal fleet identification rather than camouflage or decoration.
Use a complete front-port three-quarter view about 12 degrees above with
restrained near-orthographic perspective, 3:2 landscape, warm upper-front-port
key, cool aft rim, charcoal stars, and faint teal grid. Render as original
late-1970s/early-1980s gouache-and-airbrush technical art. No text, logo,
watermark, open tank or hangar, leak, weapon fire, planet, modern CGI, or
franchise styling.


correction subdivided the same brow into five without changing the tanker
frame. The final plate derives from that corrected anchor and uses Baltic
