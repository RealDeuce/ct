# Hanse Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 049*

## Mechanical identity

- Family: `family-49`, Hanse
- Members: `ship-49` Lübeck II, `ship-50` Hamburg II, `ship-51` Lübeck I, and
  `ship-52` Hamburg I
- Shipyard path: Path 1, Concord Exchange Yards
- Hull: 300 displacement tons / approximately 4,200 m³; standard configuration
- Shared fit: two armor points, maneuver/power drive C / thrust 2, two weeks of
  power endurance, six staterooms, workshop, repair drones, autodoc, emergency
  berth, holographic bridge controls, and one single beam-laser turret
- Shared hardpoints: one occupied beam-laser socket and two closed empty sockets

Lübeck I and II are exterior-identical TL13 Jump-2 freighters with Jump drive C
and 131.5 tons cargo. Hamburg I and II are exterior-identical TL11 system
freighters with no Jump drive and 211.5 tons cargo. The four records use one
main frame and door grid; only the Jump-service inserts distinguish Lübeck from
Hamburg.

## Canonical family architecture

The Hanse is 53.0 m long, 19.0 m wide, and 12.0 m high. It is an integrated,
non-aerodynamic standard hull with three axial volumes: a blunt low command and
crew house, a long rectangular podded cargo cassette enclosed by two deep load
frames, and a compact aft engineering block. Chamfered corners and frame voids
plausibly enclose approximately 4,200 m³.

The command house has an eight-port bridge brow, one port crew hatch, and six
small stateroom windows. The cargo cassette has exactly three equal closed
two-leaf freight shutters on the visible port side, each with the same chrome
collar and low sill. One workshop shutter and one emergency-berth panel lie
forward of the first cargo door; one repair-drone shutter lies aft of the third.

One dorsal single-turret ring carries one recessed beam-laser optic. Two further
circular hardpoint covers remain closed along the dorsal load spine. The aft
block has two square maneuver apertures, one broad power grille, six landing-pad
doors, docking guides, and standardized commercial seams.

Lübeck shows the internal Jump drive through one segmented transverse radiator
band and two low rectangular coil-service blisters, each inside a fixed chrome
service collar. Hamburg preserves those exact collar coordinates but replaces
the radiator segments and blister machinery with quiet royal-blue cargo-service
panels. It does not grow a new hull or move any cargo door.

### Invariants

All four records preserve exactly the 53.0 × 19.0 × 12.0 m three-volume hull,
command house and windows, two main load frames, three cargo doors and sills,
workshop/emergency/drone panels, three dorsal socket centers, aft drive block,
landing and docking details, camera, crop, backdrop, and lighting.

Do not add a second installed weapon, fourth cargo door, passenger deck, visible
cargo, external tank, Jump ring, glow, separate wing, or exposed landing gear.
