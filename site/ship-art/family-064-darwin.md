# Darwin Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 064*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-64`; `ship-64` Darwin
- Shipyard path: Path 4, Civic Survey Works
- Hull: 300 displacement tons / approximately 4,200 m³
- Dimensions: 56 m long, 19.5 m wide, and 12.5 m high
- Configuration: streamlined; technology level 11; non-standard design
- Protection: four whole points of crystaliron armor
- Drives: Jump-C / Jump-2, one Jump; maneuver E and power E / thrust 3
- Endurance: two weeks of power-plant fuel
- Control and complement: holographic standard bridge, basic military
  electronics, Model/3 computer, and eight crew
- Accommodation: eight staterooms, four emergency low berths, mini-galley,
  galley for eight, recreation space for twenty, and a gymnasium
- Survey support: two laboratories, one-bed medical bay, repair drones, and
  an 83-ton cargo/sample hold
- Armament: one mixed triple turret with beam laser, sandcaster, and missile
  rack; twelve standard missiles and twenty sand canisters
- Hardpoint capacity: one occupied station and two unused fire-control stations,
  represented by exactly three exterior coordinates
- Fuel limitation: no fuel processor and no recorded scoop

Darwin is a self-contained survey scout for sustained exploration. Its large
laboratories, specialist accommodation, recreation spaces, medical support,
and sample hold make it an independent field station, but it cannot process
wilderness fuel and must plan refueling around developed facilities.

## Independent survey-lantern chassis

The 56 × 19.5 × 12.5 m streamlined hull is a long “survey lantern”: blunt
prismatic command prow, deep rounded-hexagonal occupied center body, thick
integral sensor shoulders, shallow raised observation crown over the laboratory
section, deep straight expedition cargo keel, narrowed machinery waist, and
compact tapered engineering stern. The crown is occupied observation and
instrument structure, not a wing, weapon deck, or external Jump ring. The
broken envelope plausibly contains approximately 4,200 m³.

Darwin is independent of the 300-ton Verne packet-liner family. Never copy the
Verne spearhead, twelve-leaf commercial insert, three-socket dorsal row,
vehicle garage, hangar reservation, or scoop. Never copy Janus's split-mask
prow, Warden's customs shield, or Challenger's survey-compass hull. Those Civic
ships provide finish and component vocabulary only.

Eight protected panes cross the low command brow. Six broader panes glaze the
raised recreation and mission-planning crown; this is not a second bridge.
Four of eight stateroom windows are visible along the port occupancy band and
four are hidden starboard. Two separate enormous faceted dark-teal observation/
probe bays in chrome frames express the two laboratories. They are optical
working spaces, not hangars, cargo doors, weapon bays, or transparent interiors.

One closed formal airlock, two visible/two hidden plain emergency-berth panels,
one plain medical-service hatch, and one repair-drone slot complete the
occupied section. No panel carries a cross, badge, text, or emblem.

One closed 9 × 4.5 m two-leaf cargo/sample shutter dominates the lower port
expedition keel. Its continuous frame, central seam, low sill, and handling
guides serve the 83-ton hold. Darwin carries no craft and has no hangar; never
open the shutter or show cargo, vehicle, drone, or launch inside it.

## Hardpoint, weapon, and instrument map

The hull reserves exactly three turret coordinates:

1. forward upper-port shoulder ring, visible and occupied by the mixed turret;
2. lower aft-port flank/keel-lip cover, visible and closed; and
3. upper-mid starboard shoulder cover, hidden and closed.

The standard plate shows two of three coordinates but only one installed
weapon. Its common low cup has three unlike faces: one circular dark-teal beam
optic, one fine multi-port sandcaster projector, and one separate closed
armored missile-rack shutter. Ammunition remains internal. Never arm either
closed hardpoint merely to display the design's full capacity.

Two circular instrument collars, white ceramic radomes, one fine antenna grid,
small optical flats, both laboratory frames, and the observation crown form a
purposeful Civic sensor suite. None is a turret, point-defense node, missile
shutter, or gun-like barrel. Darwin has no point-defense weapons.

## Jump, drive, fuel, and armor landmarks

The internal Jump-C drive reads through one segmented transverse radiator/
service band around the aft waist and a bilateral pair of flush coil-service
blisters, one visible and one hidden. There is no external Jump ring or glow.
Three broad port maneuver apertures are visible at the thrust-three stern and
three matching starboard apertures are hidden; a separate ribbed power grille
serves the E/E machinery.

Darwin has neither fuel processors nor a recorded scoop. Do not turn an
antenna grid, cargo fitting, radiator, power grille, landing door, or optical
bay into fuel hardware. Four armor points read through two flowing protective
bands around command, laboratories, keel and engineering waist, reinforced
component frames, and overlapping accessible plates.
