# Fabius Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production singleton family 089*

## Mechanical identity

- Record: `ship-89` Fabius
- Path: Admiralty Line Works (P7)
- Hull: 1,000 displacement tons / approximately 14,000 m³; standard
- Dimensions: 78.0 m long, 33.0 m wide, and 21.0 m high
- Fit: TL11, four armor points, Jump H / Jump-2, maneuver and power H / thrust
  2, two weeks endurance, advanced electronics, holographic controls, and
  Model 3/bis
- Complement: six staterooms, five crew berthings, forty-nine crew, briefing
  room, office, armory, emergency low berth, and two-bed medical bay
- Fleet support: 344.5 tons cargo, ten underway-replenishment systems, two
  workshops, five fuel processors, scoop, repair drones, and one closed
  110-ton full hangar with no assigned carried craft
- Weapons: four triple-beam turrets, three triple-missile turrets, three triple-
  sandcaster turrets, and ten point-defense lasers; all ten hardpoints used

Fabius is an independent fleet-replenishment family. It must not reuse
Aquarius's distributed tank train or the Baltic League's 2,000-ton logistics
chassis. Its filled service-anvil hull, paired transfer galleries, protected
load rails, and formal compartment order establish a separate Admiralty design.

## Canonical architecture

The hull is a 78.0 × 33.0 × 21.0 m “fleet service anvil”: a blunt faceted
command prow, deep integrated central store-and-service citadel, two protected
longitudinal load rails, asymmetric port hangar shoulder, and broad squared
engineering stern. It is a filled wingless multi-deck spacecraft, not an
aircraft, watercraft, open truss, tank train, or detachable container carrier.

Five identical replenishment stations line the visible port citadel and five
matching stations are hidden starboard. Every station has one capped circular
transfer coupling, short folded boom, protected hose reel, and isolation valves.
All equipment is fully stowed. The couplings and reels are industrial fittings,
never weapons or turrets.

One closed ribbed 12 × 7 m hangar shutter occupies the port-aft shoulder with a
protected internal axis suitable for 110 displacement tons. The catalog assigns
no carried craft, so the plate shows none inside, outside, or docked. One smaller
closed stores shutter serves the 344.5-ton hold. Three of six stateroom windows
appear portside and three remain hidden. Workshop, medical, emergency-berth,
airlock, armory, office, and repair-drone access remains closed.

## Ten-coordinate coverage map

Ten large hardpoints divide evenly between visible and occluded hull halves.
The five visible triple turrets are distributed across upper, flank, lower,
forward, and aft arcs rather than concentrated on the crown:

1. Forward upper-port shoulder: triple-beam cup with exactly three cyan optics.
2. Dorsal-port fore-mid shoulder: second triple-beam cup with three cyan optics.
3. Midship port side beneath the hangar: triple-missile mount with three closed
   rectangular launch shutters.
4. Lower-port/ventral mid-aft: triple-sandcaster mount with three blunt square
   projectors, each showing four dark ports in a 2 × 2 array.
5. Aft port side: second triple-sandcaster mount with the same three-projector
   language.

The hidden starboard and ventral half contains two triple-beam, two triple-
missile, and one triple-sandcaster turret. The resulting network overlaps fore,
aft, upper, lower, and both flank fields with no crown-only battery.

Ten much smaller point-defense coordinates split five visible and five hidden.
Visible nodes are low octagonal red-rimmed gimbals, each with one small optic,
placed between large mounts rather than attached to transfer equipment. They
remain visibly subordinate to the triple turrets.

Transfer couplings, processor grilles, hangar and cargo latches, workshop
vents, sensors, radiator panels, Jump housings, scoop, and drive apertures are
unarmed. Missiles and sand canisters remain internal.

## Fuel, Jump, drive, and armor

Five processors use two circular ribbed port grilles and three hidden starboard
grilles, plus a separate coupling and manifold. One protected lower-port/
ventral scoop grid is an intake/field-grid region rather than a door or weapon.

The internal Jump-H drive reads through one segmented transverse radiator/
service band and two flush coil-service housings near the aft waist. There is
no exterior Jump ring, hoop, portal, glow, or tank. Two broad port maneuver
apertures and two hidden starboard apertures flank distinct power grilles at the
thrust-two stern.

Four armor points read through overlapping serviceable plates, protected edge
bands, and reinforced command, hangar, transfer, and weapon frames. Fabius is a
durable fleet auxiliary rather than a battleship citadel.

## Admiralty finish and catalog plate

- Deep navy `#203B69`: lower hull, load rails, command side fields, and
  engineering bands
- Warm white `#E8E5D4`: command crown, central store citadel, hangar shutter,
  and selected stern faces
- Signal red `#C43C35`: replenishment safety collars, weapon and PD rims,
  hangar outline, and drive-service accents
- Bright aluminum/chrome: rail edges, coupling guards, opening frames, turret
  rims, and machinery interfaces
- Gunmetal/dark blue: recesses, grilles, glazing, shutters, and apertures

Color is formal fleet recognition rather than camouflage. Use a complete
front-port three-quarter view about 12 degrees above, restrained near-
orthographic perspective, 3:2 landscape, warm upper-front-port key, cool aft
rim, charcoal stars, and faint teal grid. Render as original late-1970s/early-
1980s gouache-and-airbrush technical art. No text, logo, watermark, open door,
deployed boom, external craft, weapon fire, planet, modern CGI, or franchise
styling.

## Production sequence and provenance

Generated for Cepheus Trader on 2026-08-21 with OpenAI image generation in
built-in mode. The first neutral pass placed four of five large covers on the
crown. Two scoped anchor edits redistributed the battery and established five
visible covers across upper and flank arcs. The first production pass mistook
two processor grilles for weapons; a scoped correction restored them and left
exactly five large mounts. The two sandcaster faces were then corrected one at
a time from that approved plate so their three-projector layouts remained
legible without disturbing the beam, missile, transfer, or PD systems.
