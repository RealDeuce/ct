# Apollo Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 014*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-14`; `ship-14` Apollo
- Shipyard path: Path 7, Admiralty Line Works
- Hull: 10 displacement tons / approximately 140 m³
- Dimensions: 12.8 m long, 5.2 m wide, and 3.6 m high
- Configuration: streamlined; technology level 11; non-standard design
- Protection: unarmored
- Drives: maneuver sC and power sG / thrust 6; no Jump drive
- Endurance: one week of power-plant fuel
- Control: one pilot in a one-person cockpit; basic-military electronics,
  Model/2 computer, Evade/1, and Fire Control/1
- Capacity: no passengers, pressure airlock, or catalog cargo allocation
- Armament: one trainable single beam-laser turret; no ammunition

Apollo is a close defensive-screen fighter whose energy weapon permits repeated
sorties without missile expenditure. It remains completely dependent on a
carrier or base for protection, maintenance, recovery, and interstellar
movement. The source description's immediate stores are cockpit mission items,
not a cargo hold; the executable record allocates zero cargo and the exterior
has no freight door.

## Canonical power spindle

The 12.8 × 5.2 × 3.6 m hull is a compact faceted prolate spindle: short tapered
ogive nose, thick broad midbody, shallow integral upper and lower chines, and a
rapidly tapered truncated engineering tail. The filled spindle plausibly
encloses approximately 140 m³ while remaining longer and narrower than the
other ten-ton fighters.

The pressure body is one continuous atmospheric envelope, not a cylinder,
contemporary airplane, or missile. It has no wings, tailplanes, fins, arrowhead
shoulders, disc/almond planform, external pod, or separate fuselage. Family
independence also forbids treating Apollo as the Sigrun laser fit on Valkyrie's
arrowhead chassis: Apollo's weapon is trainable, its computer lacks fib, and
its hull and power architecture are separate.

Exactly five dark panes form one low wraparound pilot visor. Its canopy seam is
the only personnel access. A dark flush strip across the upper nose is a
barrel-free military sensor. There is no pressure airlock, side door, passenger
window, cargo shutter, armor band, or layered plate. Six flush landing-pad
seams, recovery sockets, tie-downs, and fine maintenance panels remain human-
scale.

## Lower-port beam gimbal

The one circular hardpoint ring occupies the lower port shoulder/chin beneath
and slightly behind the cockpit, canted outward and downward rather than placed
on the dorsal crown. One compact hemispherical trainable cup carries exactly
one short narrow cyan optical beam emitter in one recessed aperture.

The side/underside placement provides forward, port, ventral, and much of the
aft field while the fighter maneuvers as part of a screen. One mount cannot
eliminate the far-starboard and upper blind regions. Do not invent a dorsal
partner, second optic, point-defense lens, missile shutter, or gun-like sensor.
The small signal-red fittings elsewhere are solid recovery/service marks with
no lens, aperture, or weapon function.

## Drives and Admiralty recognition

Four compact rectangular maneuver-field apertures surround one distinct large
cruciform ribbed power grille at the truncated stern. The grille's visual
dominance expresses the sG plant that supports the beam weapon and six-gravity
performance. There is no scoop, processor, Jump radiator, coil housing, ring,
external tank, flame, or glow.

Admiralty Line Works uses deep navy `#203B69`, warm white `#E8E5D4`, signal red
`#C43C35`, and bright aluminum/chrome. Navy covers the lower spindle, aft
engineering volume, and selected longitudinal panels; white defines the
observation nose, visor surround, and orderly upper recognition band; red marks
the turret collar, recovery points, and drive safety details; aluminum protects
the structural chines, canopy, hardpoint, sensor, landing, and machinery
interfaces. The bright longitudinal trim is structure and datum marking, not
armor.
