# Jutland Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 210*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-210`; `ship-210` Jutland
- Shipyard path: Path 7, Admiralty Line Works
- Hull: 5,000 displacement tons / approximately 70,000 m³
- Dimensions: 168 m long, 58 m wide, and 35 m high
- Configuration: standard; technology level 14; standard design
- Protection: twelve bonded-superdense armor points and stealth treatment
- Drives: Jump Z / Jump-2; maneuver Z and power Z / thrust 2
- Endurance: one Jump-2 and four weeks of power-plant fuel
- Control: very-advanced electronics and fib Model/6 computer
- Personnel/support: 223 crew, 101 staterooms, 60-person barracks, 223 low
  berths, 56 emergency low berths, 223 escape pods, six armories, 54 fuel
  processors, scoop, and 635 tons fleet stores
- Flight facilities: one 300-ton internal hangar carrying twenty Thermopylae
  fighters (`ship-200`) and two Proteus Modular cutters (`ship-209`)
- Main battery: ten fusion-gun bays and five missile banks
- Secondary battery: thirty-five triple-beam turrets
- Defense: one meson screen and one nuclear damper
- Ammunition: 3,600 smart missiles

Jutland is an independent standard-hull capital family. It shares formal
Admiralty component language with other fleet ships, but not Trafalgar's
streamlined battle-carrier wedge. Jutland is a blunt three-tier fortress slab
whose weapon shoulders, flight block, screen belts, stores volume, and square
engineering stern remain visibly separate systems.

## Canonical fortress hull

The 168 × 58 × 35 m hull combines a blunt rectangular faceted command prow,
massive central citadel, broad stepped weapon shoulders, deep ventral
keel/lip, integrated flight shoulders, pinched service waist, and enormous
square stern. Twelve-point bonded-superdense armor uses deep overlapping
faceted slabs, protected recesses, short load paths, and low-sheen stealth
panel interiors. An eight-pane buried bridge, paired advanced-sensor faces,
and one barrel-free command/sensor crown establish command scale.

One enormous closed lower-midship shutter serves the 635-ton stores hold. Four
protected waist airlocks, grouped escape-pod hatch banks, accommodation and
barracks service bands, six armory panels, landing doors, and handholds remain
distinct from every weapon, flight, screen, and machinery feature. Pod and
processor arrays are grouped service banks rather than an attempt to paint
hundreds of equal giant doors.

## Capital-bay map

Fifteen capital bays use an eight-visible/seven-hidden split:

- five visible fusion-gun bays occupy forward upper-port shoulder, forward
  port flank, mid upper shoulder, mid lower port flank, and aft port flank;
- five fusion partners are hidden on starboard/deeper structures;
- three visible missile banks occupy forward lower-port, mid upper-port, and
  aft upper-port shoulders; and
- two missile-bank partners are hidden.

Each visible fusion bay is a deep 13–16 m multi-deck heat-shielded throat with
one broad recessed focusing mouth and non-glowing field collars. Each missile
bank is a much larger closed rectangular grid of armored launch shutters; no
cell is open and no missile is exposed. All bays remain dramatically larger
than a 3–4 m turret cup. The stores shutter, six flight shutters, screen belts,
processor banks, and stern grilles never count as bays.

## Distributed turret coverage

Thirty-five trainable triple-beam turrets prioritize overlapping arcs and
minimal blind spots. The port three-quarter plate exposes exactly eighteen in
six spatially separated groups of three:

1. forward upper-port shoulder;
2. mid/aft upper shoulder;
3. forward port cheek/flank;
4. mid/lower port flank;
5. port ventral/lower lip; and
6. aft port quarter.

Each cup uses one low white rectangular housing on a signal-red circular base,
with exactly three clearly countable vertical cyan optical slits. The remaining
seventeen stations are hidden across starboard shoulders and flank, deeper
ventral keel, far forward cheeks, and far aft quarters. The complete battery
therefore covers dorsal, ventral, port, starboard, forward, and stern sectors
without migrating onto a roof row. There are no point-defense nodes, barbettes,
extra circular mounts, or decorative guns.

## Internal flight complex

One 300-ton internal hangar uses six standardized closed Admiralty flight
shutters. Four connect to deeper five-fighter banks for twenty canonical
11.8 × 6.4 × 3.2 m Thermopylae fighters. Two connect to at least 28 m
internal axes for canonical 25.5 × 8.4 × 5 m Proteus Modular cutters. A common
exterior shutter size simplifies armor and launch handling; internal cell
length and organization distinguish fighter banks from cutter cells.

All twenty-two craft remain internal and invisible. The six shutters belong to
one custom hangar, not six construction components, external clamps, cargo
doors, missile banks, or maneuver apertures. Never open a shutter, show a
hangar interior, place craft on the hull, or repaint an exposed cutter in
Admiralty colors.

## Screens, drives, and fuel

The meson screen appears as one continuous segmented non-glowing field-emitter
belt around the mid citadel. The nuclear damper is a separate paired aft
grid/collar array. Neither carries a barrel, aiming aperture, or turret ring.

Jump Z / Jump-2 uses two parallel non-glowing segmented upper-aft radiator
bands and four large paired coil/service housings. Six huge stern field
apertures and four power grilles identify the two-gravity Z/Z plant. One broad
lower port/ventral scoop field and several protected banks totaling 54 small
processor segments support refueling. Never add a glowing Jump ring, exposed
tank, flame, second scoop, or treat machinery boxes as turrets.

## Admiralty finish and invariants

Deep navy `#203B69` covers lower armor, citadel, and stealth fields; warm white
`#E8E5D4` marks command planes, upper datum terraces, screen housings, and
selected flight/cargo panels; signal red `#C43C35` defines weapon, magazine,
flight, cargo, and drive boundaries; bright aluminum/chrome protects rings,
bay frames, screen edges, Jump/fuel/drive collars, and datum rails. Gunmetal
remains inside deep recesses. This is formal fleet identification, not gray
camouflage.
