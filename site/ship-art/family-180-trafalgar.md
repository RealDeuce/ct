# Trafalgar Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 180*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-180`; `ship-180` Trafalgar
- Hull: 5,000 displacement tons / approximately 70,000 m³
- Dimensions: 160 m long, 56 m wide, and 32 m high
- Configuration: streamlined; technology level 11
- Protection: ten crystaliron armor points, radiation shielding, and armored
  bulkheads around bridge, drives, and ordnance magazines
- Drives: Jump-Z / Jump-2, maneuver Z, and power Z
- Endurance: one Jump and three weeks of power-plant fuel
- Control: advanced electronics, command/hardened holographic bridge, two
  fib/bis Model/3 computers, emergency power, and improved magazine
- Personnel/support: 297 crew, twelve-bed ward, operating theater, four
  workshops, powered-armor support, twenty PD-drone systems, and 959.1 tons of
  cargo/stores
- Boat facilities: one 100-ton full hangar carrying one Boreas (`ship-176`),
  one Zephyrus (`ship-177`), and two Castors (`ship-178`)
- Main bays: two 100-ton torpedo bays, six meson-gun bays, and six particle-
  beam bays
- Barbettes: eight plasma and six railgun
- Turrets: twelve triple-beam and ten mixed missile/missile/sand
- Point defense: fifty laser nodes
- Ammunition: 12 torpedoes, 576 railgun rounds, 1,440 missiles, and 400 sand
  canisters
- Fuel handling: no scoop and no processor

## Canonical hull

Trafalgar occupies a 160 × 56 × 32 m long flattened armored battle-carrier
wedge. A blunt tapered command prow, deep central citadel, broad terraced weapon
shoulders, strong ventral lip and keel, pinched service waist, and enormous
rectangular engineering battery establish capital scale. The approximately
70,000 m³ enclosed volume remains credible without turning the ship into an
unbounded city.

Ten-point armor uses deep overlapping edge layers, thick shoulder slabs,
protected recesses, a radiation seam band, and short armored load paths around
command, drives, and magazines. The command bridge is buried beneath the prow
brow with exactly eight narrow panes and paired advanced-sensor faces. The hull
has no wings or decorative fins.

## Capital-bay map

Fourteen capital bays occupy seven bilateral pairs. The port three-quarter
plate exposes exactly seven; seven starboard mirrors are hidden.

- Three visible meson-gun bays run along the forward/upper port shoulder. Each
  is a multi-deck dark focusing throat with layered non-glowing field vanes.
- Three visible particle-beam bays run along the mid/lower port shoulder. Each
  is a deep insulated axial channel with one massive recessed emitter and heat
  collars.
- One visible aft/lower port torpedo bay has exactly six large closed shutters
  in two rows of three. Its starboard twin accounts for the second bay and the
  twelve-round total load.

Every approximately 13 × 5 m bay aperture is much larger and deeper than a
barbette or turret. Meson and particle architecture must remain distinct. No
bay is allowed to merge with the cargo door or hangar.

## Barbette map

Fourteen barbettes occupy seven complementary pairs. The plate shows exactly
seven: four plasma and three railgun. The seven counterparts are occluded on
far or lower surfaces.

Visible plasma positions span forward port flank, upper shoulder, lower
shoulder, and aft port flank. Each 7–10 m fairing ends in a short broad heat-
shielded projector mouth. Visible railgun positions span forward-lower port,
mid port flank, and aft-upper port. Each carries one recessed linear accelerator
and heavy recoil structure.

Barbettes are substantially larger than the standard one-ton turret cup but
smaller than a multi-deck capital bay. They live between the bays and across
upper, lateral, and lower structures rather than forming a dorsal row.

## Turret map and overlapping coverage

Twenty-two triple-turret rings form eleven complementary pairs. The standard
view shows exactly eleven stations and hides eleven:

| Fit | Visible stations | Hidden stations |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Triple beam | 6 | 6 |
| Mixed missile/missile/sand | 5 | 5 |

The six visible beam cups occupy forward dorsal, mid dorsal, upper port flank,
aft dorsal, port ventral lip, and aft port flank. Each uses three narrow optical
emitters. The five visible mixed cups occupy forward port shoulder, forward
port flank, mid lower port flank, aft port shoulder, and stern-upper port
quarter. Each uses two closed missile shutters and one blunt sandcaster
aperture.

The hidden half covers starboard shoulders, the deeper ventral surface, and
far-side forward/aft quarters. Together the twenty-two stations overlap every
hemisphere and minimize axial and lower blind spots. Preserve this map: the
plate must not promote hidden mounts into view, expose more than eleven, or
move flank/ventral-lip stations to the crown for compositional convenience.

## Point-defense map

Fifty laser nodes form twenty-five complementary pairs. Exactly twenty-five
tiny blue-white lenses appear as five groups of five on prow armor, forward
dorsal shoulder, mid port flank, aft dorsal shoulder, and port ventral lip. The
twenty-five partners are hidden on far and lower surfaces.

Each lens is 0.4–0.8 m, dramatically smaller than a turret ring. PD groups must
read as distributed defensive optics, never missile cells, windows, or small
decorative dorsal guns.

## Cargo, boats, and access

The enormous closed lower-midship fleet-stores shutter serves the 959.1-ton
hold. It is ribbed, heavily framed, and unarmed. A separate closed aft-port full-
hangar shutter has at least 30 m clear internal axis and enough beam to berth the
20-ton Boreas, 20-ton Zephyrus, and two 30-ton Castors. All four boats remain
inside; no craft appears beside Trafalgar in the catalog plate.

Four protected port-waist airlocks, repair-drone slots, powered-armor service
interfaces, landing doors, and handholds remain distinct from both large
shutters and all weapon apertures.

## Drives and fuel recognition

Jump-Z uses two long parallel non-glowing segmented radiator bands and four
large paired armored coil/service housings along the upper-aft spine. The
boxlike housings are machinery, not additional turrets, and carry no aiming
optics or barrels. Six huge recessed maneuver apertures and four ribbed power
grilles fill the protected Z/Z stern.

Trafalgar has neither a scoop nor fuel processors. Do not add a ventral intake,
processing grille, exposed tank, or generic glowing Jump ring.

## Admiralty finish

Admiralty Line Works uses deep navy `#203B69`, warm white `#E8E5D4`, signal
red `#C43C35`, and bright aluminum. Warm white marks command and upper datum
planes; navy covers lower armor and citadel fields; red defines weapon,
magazine, hangar, and hazard boundaries; aluminum protects rings, collars,
frames, and longitudinal datum lines. The color is formal fleet identification,
not camouflage or modern gray default rendering.
