# Greyhound Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production singleton family 092*

## Mechanical identity

- Record: `ship-92` Greyhound
- Path: Admiralty Line Works (P7)
- Hull: 600 displacement tons / approximately 8,400 m³; standard
- Dimensions: 72.0 m long, 22.0 m wide, and 16.0 m high
- Fit: TL11, four armor points, Jump H / Jump-2, maneuver and power V / thrust
  6, two weeks endurance, advanced electronics, hardened holographic bridge,
  and Model 3/bis
- Complement: five staterooms, two crew berthings, twenty-six crew, briefing
  room, office, armory, four-cell brig, two emergency low berths, and two-bed
  medical bay
- Patrol support: 127 tons cargo, eight fuel processors, scoop, probe drones,
  repair drones, and one Venture Caduceus in a 20-ton full hangar
- Weapons: two triple-beam turrets, one triple-missile turret, one triple-
  sandcaster turret, two particle-beam barbettes, and six point-defense lasers;
  all six large hardpoints used

Greyhound is an independent fast-patrol family. It shares formal Admiralty
component and finish language with Cook but not Cook's blunt rectangular
chassis. Its long pursuit prow, narrow boarding waist, deep mission keel, and
oversized thrust-six stern establish a separate high-acceleration design.

## Canonical architecture

The hull is a 72.0 × 22.0 × 16.0 m “pursuit cleaver”: a deep clipped command
wedge with recessed seven-pane bridge brow, narrow protected sensor-and-
boarding waist, deep central cargo/mission keel, asymmetric port boat shoulder,
and disproportionately broad engineering stern. It is a filled wingless
standard spacecraft, not an aircraft, watercraft, smoothly streamlined clipper,
open truss, or detachable module train.

One closed 7.2 × 5.2 m two-leaf full-hangar shutter occupies the aft port
shoulder. Its approximately 19 m internal axis clears the approved 17.5 × 6.4 ×
4.6 m Venture Caduceus, which remains internal. A separate large closed shutter
serves the 127-ton hold. Three of five stateroom windows appear portside and two
remain hidden. Boarding airlock and docking collar, brig, armory, medical,
emergency-berth, probe-drone, repair-drone, and office panels remain closed.

## Six-coordinate coverage map

The four ordinary triple-turret coordinates and two distinct particle-barbette
coordinates form six large weapon stations. Four are visible and two are
occluded—an intentional fore/aft asymmetry that exposes every weapon type:

1. Forward upper-port shoulder: triple-beam turret with exactly three cyan
   optics.
2. Midship upper-port shoulder before the hangar: triple-missile turret with
   exactly three closed rectangular launch shutters.
3. Lower-aft port flank beneath the hangar: triple-sandcaster turret with
   exactly three blunt square projectors, each showing four dark ports in a
   2 × 2 array.
4. Lower-forward port cheek: one long low 8 m semi-recessed particle-beam
   barbette with one heavy insulated blue-white emitter mouth.

The hidden starboard half contains the second triple-beam turret and second
particle-beam barbette. The visible weapons cover forward, upper, lower, flank,
and aft arcs rather than forming a dorsal row. The barbette channel is longer
and heavier than a turret but far smaller than a 51-ton weapon bay.

Six point-defense coordinates divide three visible and three hidden. Visible
nodes are tiny signal-red-rimmed single-optic gimbals on separated fore, waist,
and aft arcs. They remain less than one quarter of a turret-base diameter.

The two large flush circular disks on the far-aft crown are Jump coil-service
housings, not weapons. Processor grilles, sensors, bridge panes, hangar and
cargo latches, scoop, docking machinery, radiator bands, and drive apertures
also remain unarmed. Missiles and sand canisters remain internal.

## Fuel, Jump, drive, and armor

Eight processors use four circular ribbed port grilles and four hidden
starboard grilles, with separate coupling and manifold. One protected ventral
scoop grid is an intake/field-grid region rather than a hangar or weapon.

The internal Jump-H drive reads through one segmented transverse radiator/
service band and the two flush crown coil-service housings. There is no exterior
Jump ring, hoop, portal, glow, or tank. Three broad port maneuver apertures and
three hidden starboard apertures flank two power grilles at the deep thrust-six
stern.

Four armor points read through overlapping serviceable plates, hardened bridge
visor, reinforced hangar and weapon frames, and protected edge bands. The ship
remains a fast patrol frigate rather than a line-of-battle citadel.

## Admiralty finish and catalog plate

- Deep navy `#203B69`: lower prow, cargo keel, waist rails, and engineering side
  fields
- Warm white `#E8E5D4`: command crown, upper mission body, closed cargo and
  hangar shutters, and selected stern faces
- Signal red `#C43C35`: weapon and PD collars, hangar outline, rescue/airlock
  accents, and drive-service boundaries
- Bright aluminum/chrome: edge ribs, bridge visor, opening frames, turret rims,
  barbette insulation, and machinery interfaces
- Gunmetal/dark blue: recesses, glazing, grilles, shutters, and apertures

Use a complete front-port three-quarter view about 12 degrees above, restrained
near-orthographic perspective, 3:2 landscape, warm upper-front-port key, cool
aft rim, charcoal stars, and faint teal grid. Render as original late-1970s/
early-1980s gouache-and-airbrush technical art with bright fleet enamel and
selective chrome. No text, logo, watermark, open door, external craft, weapon
fire, planet, modern CGI, or franchise styling.

## Production sequence and provenance

Generated for Cepheus Trader on 2026-08-21 with OpenAI image generation in
built-in mode. The neutral singleton anchor was accepted without correction.
The first production pass preserved the distributed battery and clearly
separated the fixed barbette, PD nodes, and Jump housings, but produced four
missile boxes and two sandcaster heads. Two scoped single-object edits corrected
those mounts to exact triple configurations without moving any hull, hangar,
drive, or other weapon feature.
