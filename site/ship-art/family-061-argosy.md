# Argosy Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 061*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-61`; `ship-61` Argosy
- Shipyard path: Path 2, Venture Passage Works
- Hull: 400 displacement tons / approximately 5,600 m³
- Dimensions: 68 m long, 23 m wide, and 14.2 m high
- Configuration: streamlined; technology level 11; non-standard design
- Protection: four whole points of crystaliron armor
- Drives: Jump-D / Jump-2, one Jump; maneuver D and power D / thrust 2
- Endurance: two weeks of power-plant fuel
- Control and complement: standard bridge, basic military electronics,
  Model/3 computer, and five crew
- Accommodation: seven staterooms, ten low berths, and a one-bed medical bay
- Freight and route support: 182 tons cargo and two fuel processors
- Armament: one mixed triple turret with beam laser, missile rack, and
  sandcaster; twelve standard missiles and twenty sand canisters
- Hardpoint capacity: one occupied station and three unused fire-control
  stations, represented by exactly four exterior hardpoint coordinates

Argosy is a starting merchant freighter. Its passenger facilities and fuel
processing support long frontier routes, but the 182-ton hold dominates the
ship and the lone mixed turret remains a deterrent rather than a warship fit.

## Independent cargo-whale chassis

The 68 × 23 × 14.2 m streamlined hull is a “cargo whale”: blunt rounded-
dolphin command prow, thick occupied shoulders, one enormous deep trapezoidal
cargo citadel with clipped aerodynamic corners, two heavy transverse
protective arches, a shallow upper occupancy ridge, deep belly, narrowed aft
waist, and compact tapered engineering stern. The broken outline plausibly
contains approximately 5,600 m³.

Argosy is independent of the Verne and Hanse families. Never copy Verne's
50 × 18 × 11 m packet-liner spearhead, twelve-leaf commercial insert, three-
socket dorsal row, vehicle garage, hangar reservation, or scoop. Never copy
Hanse's non-aerodynamic three-volume frame or three equal cargo shutters.

Exactly seven bridge panes cross the command brow. Four of seven stateroom
windows are visible on the port upper ridge; three are hidden starboard. Six
of ten low-berth service panels occupy the port corridor above the cargo hold;
four are hidden starboard. This mild accommodation asymmetry is intentional.
One large closed personnel airlock and one smaller plain medical-service hatch
sit forward. The medical hatch carries no cross, badge, text, or emblem.

One continuous chrome load frame contains the hold's enormous closed
24 × 7 m four-leaf freight shutter. Three vertical divisions express four
leaves without becoming four independent holds. The shutter remains closed;
no cargo, gantry, vehicle, or interior is visible.

## Hardpoint and weapon map

The hull reserves exactly four hardpoint coordinates:

1. forward upper-port shoulder ring, visible and occupied by the mixed turret;
2. lower-aft port flank/ventral-lip cover, visible and closed;
3. mid upper-starboard cover, hidden by the hull; and
4. lower-forward starboard/ventral cover, hidden by the hull.

The standard front-port plate therefore exposes two of four coordinates,
approximately half the total. The occupied upper-flank station contains three
unlike apertures in one common low cup: one narrow blue beam optic, one closed
missile shutter, and one blunt sandcaster projector. The lower visible station
and both hidden stations are empty. Never turn a closed cover, processor,
coupling, airlock detail, bridge sensor, or cargo latch into another weapon.
All ammunition remains internal.

The upper-flank and lower-flank distribution begins the overlapping-field
logic used on heavily armed ships without falsely clustering every hardpoint
on the dorsal centerline. Argosy has only one installed turret, so no extra
weapons may be added merely to make the plate look balanced.

## Fuel, Jump, drive, and armor landmarks

One square processor grille and adjacent circular refueling coupling appear
on the lower aft port flank; the second processor is hidden starboard. Argosy
has no recorded fuel scoop. Keep the processors distinct from the hardpoint
cover and never invent a belly scoop.

Jump-D / Jump-2 is expressed by two segmented transverse service bands around
the aft waist and four oval coil/service blisters, two visible and two hidden.
The drive is internal: there is no external ring, luminous portal, or glow.
One of two broad maneuver apertures is visible at the stern, with a separate
central ribbed power grille; the matching maneuver aperture is hidden.

Four armor points read through long protected edge rails, the two transverse
arches, overlapping belly plates, and reinforced cargo and command frames.
These are integrated protection, not detachable tanks or arbitrary ribs.
