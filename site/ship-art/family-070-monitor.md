# Monitor Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 070*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-70`; `ship-70` Monitor
- Shipyard path: Path 8, Redoubt Shipbuilding
- Hull: 300 displacement tons / approximately 4,200 m³
- Dimensions: 44 m long, 22 m wide, and 15 m high
- Configuration: standard; technology level 10; non-standard design
- Protection: one crystaliron layer / four armor points plus radiation shielding
- Drives: maneuver F and power F / thrust 4; no Jump drive
- Endurance: four weeks of power-plant fuel
- Control and complement: hardened standard bridge, basic military electronics,
  four fib Model/2 computers, and fourteen crew
- Accommodation and survival: two staterooms, two crew berthings, one emergency
  low berth, fourteen escape pods, gymnasium, recreation, and repair drones
- Working fit: 99.5 tons cargo; no hangar, carried craft, processor, or scoop
- Main weapons: one 51-ton particle-beam bay, one railgun barbette, one mixed
  beam/missile/sand triple turret, and three point-defense nodes
- Ammunition: 36 standard missiles, 20 sand canisters, and 80 railgun rounds

Monitor is an in-system gunboat, escort, and interceptor. Its radiation-
shielded casemate, redundant controls, complete escape capacity, four-week
endurance, useful relief/evidence hold, and layered weapons make it a compact
naval combatant. With no Jump drive it depends on a carrier, tender, or base.

## Independent armored-casemate ram

The 44 × 22 × 15 m standard hull is a compact “armored casemate ram”: blunt
near-vertical shield prow, deeply buried command block, enormous offset forward-
port weapon shoulder, thick rectangular radiation-shielded citadel, deep lower
cargo/store volume, and short oversized thrust-four stern. Chamfered handling
corners do not make it aerodynamic. The approximately 29-percent filled bounding
volume plausibly contains about 4,200 m³.

Monitor is independent of every other Redoubt combatant. Never copy Trident's
small streamlined escort citadel, Condottiere's converted Hawkwood hull,
Bulwark, Cerberus, or another ship merely because a weapon type overlaps.

Six narrow panes form the final deeply recessed bridge brow behind the shield
prow. One of two stateroom windows is visible and one hidden starboard; crew
berthing is windowless. One closed personnel airlock, one plain emergency-
berth panel, one gym/recreation service hatch, and one repair-drone slot remain
unmarked.

Exactly seven identical closed escape-pod doors occupy one protected port-side
row, with seven matching doors hidden starboard. Each is human scale and
individually bone-framed. They are not cabin windows, weapon shutters, point-
defense nodes, cargo panels, or decorative armor.

One closed 9 × 4 m two-leaf cargo/relief-store shutter lies low on the port
citadel. It serves the 99.5-ton hold and remains separate from the forward
particle bay. There is no hangar or carried craft.

## Main battery architecture and coverage

The three hardpoint-consuming installations occupy complementary positions:

1. visible forward lower-port shoulder: one 51-ton particle-beam bay;
2. visible upper aft-port shoulder: one mixed triple turret; and
3. hidden lower-aft starboard flank: one railgun barbette.

The particle bay is 15 m long × 5 m high, occupies roughly 714 m³, and visibly
shapes the prow and citadel. Thick longitudinal load paths, a deep feed trunk,
two insulated field collars, cooling/service panels, and one heavy recessed
axial emitter terminate in a single dark forward aperture. It is hull
architecture, not an enlarged turret. There is one emitter only and no glow,
beam, smoke, or external ammunition.

The visible triple cup contains exactly three unlike faces in one housing: one
narrow beam-laser optic, one closed rectangular missile-rack shutter, and one
blunt four-port sandcaster projector. The hidden 8 × 4 m railgun barbette has
parallel rails and bracing internally but no part of it is visible in the
front-port plate. It must not migrate to the crown for display.

Three point-defense positions supplement the battery:

- visible forward upper-port cheek: one tiny short multi-barrel minigun node;
- visible lower-aft port flank: one tiny clean optical laser node; and
- hidden mid-starboard shoulder: the second minigun node.

Each node is 0.4–0.8 m and much smaller than a turret ring. Never arm a pod
latch, bridge detail, sensor, cargo fitting, support pad, or drive grille.

## Drive, shielding, and service landmarks

Monitor has no Jump drive. There is no radiator band, coil housing, external
ring, tank, luminous portal, or glow. Four broad maneuver apertures are visible
in the port stern bank and four matching starboard apertures hidden; two
separate power grilles serve F/F thrust-four machinery and four-week tankage.

There are no fuel processors and no scoop. Radiation shielding and four armor
points read through three nested prow/citadel shells, thick neutron-shadow
plates around bridge and weapon feed, overlapping stainless guards, protected
escape-pod recesses, and deep component reveals.

- Particle-bay armor, weapon and PD collars, drive and recovery: orange
  `#E9782E`
- Buried bridge surround, escape-pod doors, airlock, cargo shutter and selected
  shadow plates: bone `#D8CCA9`
- Prow/citadel guards, bay load paths/collars, frames, docking and drive
  interfaces: heavy bright stainless steel
- Glazing, apertures, recesses, seams, slats and grilles: gunmetal/dark teal

Triton supplies Redoubt finish vocabulary only. Monitor retains its standard
casemate hull, architectural bay, hidden railgun barbette, pod row, cargo hold,
no-Jump stern, and exact PD map.
