# Scheherazade Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 037*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-37`; `ship-37` Scheherazade
- Shipyard path: Path 1, Concord Exchange Yards
- Hull: 100 displacement tons / approximately 1,400 m³
- Dimensions: 36 m long, 12 m wide, and 7.2 m high
- Configuration: streamlined; technology level 12; non-standard design
- Protection: one crystaliron layer / four armor points
- Drives: Jump-A / Jump-2, one Jump; maneuver B and power B / thrust 4
- Endurance: two weeks of power-plant fuel
- Control: standard bridge with holographic controls, basic civilian
  electronics, Model/3 computer, and six crew
- Accommodation: four staterooms, two high-class staterooms for four guests,
  crew recreation, galley space, autodoc, and one emergency low berth
- Mission support: one repair-drone installation and only 0.5 tons cargo
- Armament: one double turret with two beam lasers and one independent
  point-defense laser; no ammunition

Scheherazade is a compact personal yacht, executive transport, and courier. Its
two-parsec reach, four-gravity acceleration, protected suites, and defensive
fit support direct travel between surface ports. The half-ton locker is useful
for baggage and sensitive dispatches, not commerce; never give this yacht a
merchant freight shutter.

## Canonical executive-arrow chassis

The 36 × 12 × 7.2 m streamlined hull is a deep faceted stepped lozenge or
“executive arrow”: narrow clipped cut-water prow, broad occupied swept
shoulders, raised compact salon/cabin citadel, armored lower keel, and tapered
short boat-tail engineering stern. Its forward-rising and aft-descending steps,
cut-gem facets, and balanced modular pressure volumes distinguish it from
Cleopatra's broad flattened almond. The shoulders are occupied hull, not wings.
The filled envelope plausibly encloses approximately 1,400 m³.

Exactly four dark panes form the protected command brow. On the visible port
side, exactly two large high-class suite observation bays share one formal
bright-metal frame. Exactly two much smaller crew-cabin ports lie aft; they are
the visible half of four standard-cabin positions, with the starboard pair
occluded. One closed formal boarding hatch sits on the lower forward port
flank. A separate small closed 1.2 m square service-locker hatch represents the
half-ton hold. One closed three-shutter repair-drone hive lies aft of the cabin
ports; no drone is exposed.

Four armor points appear as two flowing interlocking bright-metal bands around
the prow, shoulders, occupancy frame, and lower keel, supported by overlapping
accessible plates. Flush commercial sensor strips, landing-pad doors, tie-downs,
handholds, docking collars, and maintenance panels preserve human scale.

## Fixed weapon map and coverage

One 3–4 m ring on the upper port shoulder, clearly off the dorsal centerline
and canted outward, carries one compact trainable double turret. Its shared low
housing has exactly two short narrow parallel dark-teal/cyan beam-laser optics;
these are two weapons in one turret, not two turrets.

One separate 0.5 m point-defense laser node occupies the lower aft port cheek
near the keel. It has exactly one dark-teal/cyan lens and no barrel. The low-aft
station complements the main turret's port, forward, dorsal, and aft coverage
rather than becoming a decorative crown fixture.

The standard port three-quarter plate therefore shows one of one turret and
one of one point-defense node. There are zero hidden weapon stations. Do not
add a starboard mate, dorsal mount, ventral turret, missile shutter, sandcaster,
gun-like sensor, or second defense lens.

## Jump, drives, and fuel

Jump-A / Jump-2 is identified by one compact segmented transverse radiator
across the aft upper step and paired low embedded field-service housings ahead
of the stern. It has no external ring or glowing coil. Four maneuver-field
apertures form two paired sides at the boat-tail: the plate shows the two port
apertures while the two starboard apertures are occluded. Distinct smaller
ribbed power-service grilles support the B/B plant.

The design fits no fuel scoop or processor. Preserve the closed belly with only
landing, defense, and ordinary service landmarks. Never add an intake grid,
processor bank, external tank, flame, or drive glow.

## Concord Exchange recognition finish

- Principal occupied pressure body: warm ivory `#F1E3C2`
- Command and accommodation band: royal blue `#2354A3`
- Prow, access, weapon collars, repair shutters, and datum accents: vermilion
  `#E44B2D`
- Armor bands, glazing frames, docking and machinery interfaces: polished
  aluminum/chrome
- Recesses and grilles: gunmetal
- Glazing and laser optics: very dark teal with restrained cyan highlights

The uninterrupted blue occupancy datum, orderly spacing, rectangular chrome
collars, faired machinery, and bright maintained finish establish Concord
Exchange lineage. Cleopatra is a finish reference only. Equal tonnage, role,
and shipyard do not license its almond silhouette, five-pane brow, six-window
salon, covered dorsal hardpoint, or machinery map.

## Production sequence and provenance

1. Generate the independent neutral executive-arrow chassis with fixed
   accommodation, access, defense, Jump, and machinery coordinates.
2. Correct only the bridge partitions until exactly four panes remain.
3. Save that corrected result as the family anchor.
4. Derive the production fit from the anchor, using Cleopatra only for Concord
   color/material language and fitting weapons only in the two planned sockets.
5. Verify the one-visible/zero-hidden turret and one-visible/zero-hidden
   point-defense maps before web conversion.
