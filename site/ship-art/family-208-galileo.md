# Galileo Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 208*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-208`; `ship-208` Galileo
- Shipyard path: Path 4, Civic Survey Works
- Hull: 300 displacement tons / approximately 4,200 m³
- Dimensions: 59 m long, 21 m wide, and 14.5 m high
- Configuration: standard; technology level 11; standard design
- Protection: two titanium-steel armor points
- Drives: Jump B / Jump-1; maneuver C and power C / thrust 2
- Endurance: two Jump-1 fuel loads and four weeks of power-plant fuel
- Mission support: 14 crew, eight staterooms, four emergency low berths, six
  laboratories, four probe-drone systems, four processors, scoop, and 39 tons
  expedition cargo
- Boat facilities: two lifeboat hangars, each carrying one Dory (`ship-194`)
- Armament: three triple-beam turrets; all three hardpoints occupied

Galileo shares Civic Survey's component grammar and institutional finish with
Bacon, but not Bacon's hull. It is a separate armed survey design with greater
endurance, acceleration, crew, probe capacity, and defensive coverage. The
dominant ringed observatory core, bilateral boat shoulders, and deep fuel keel
must never be replaced by a stretched version of Bacon's tiered barge.

## Canonical observatory ship

The 59 × 21 × 14.5 m standard hull integrates a clipped command prow, thick
ringed central observatory/pressure core, short bilateral mission shoulders,
deep expedition/fuel keel, and compact squared stern. The sponsons remain
joined to one pressure hull; Galileo is not a distributed frame or atmospheric
aircraft. One protected edge band expresses its light armor.

A six-pane bridge brow and four small port stateroom panes establish occupied
scale. Three visible barrel-free instrument modules around the core represent
the port half of six laboratories; their starboard partners are hidden. Each
uses dark-teal optical flats, heat panels, sample drawers, and sensor grids.
The stepped circular observatory and its central glazed cap are laboratory and
sensor architecture, never a turret, weapon bay, docking ring, or spacecraft
carried on the hull.

Two small visible lime probe shutters serve the port half of four probe-drone
systems; their partners are hidden. All remain closed and no drone is visible.
One separate closed cyan shutter serves the 39-ton expedition/sample hold.
Emergency-berth access, specimen-transfer fittings, sensor blisters, antenna
flats, and handholds remain human-scale and unarmed.

## Bilateral Dory cells

Two lifeboat hangars occupy matching mission shoulders. The plate exposes one
closed port shutter approximately 8.5 × 5.5 m with at least a 16 m internal
axis for one canonical 14.5 × 7.5 × 4.5 m Dory. The equal starboard shutter
and second Dory are occluded. Both launches remain internal and invisible.
Never add an external clamp, expose either craft, duplicate the visible door,
or confuse the expedition-cargo shutter with a second port launch cell.

## Hardpoint and firing-arc map

Three trainable triple-beam hardpoints cover complementary axes:

- one visible forward upper-port shoulder turret carries exactly three short
  optical emitters;
- one visible aft lower-port flank/ventral-lip turret carries exactly three
  short optical emitters; and
- one hidden mid starboard/ventral turret carries the third triple-beam fit.

The standard port view therefore exposes two of three turrets. The upper and
lower visible stations overlap forward, port, dorsal, ventral, and aft arcs;
the hidden station closes the starboard and lower blind region. Never put all
three on the observatory crown, expose the hidden turret, add a fourth ring,
or interpret lab optics, probe shutters, and fuel-service fittings as weapons.

## Drive, fuel, and Civic Survey recognition

Jump B / Jump-1 uses one medium non-glowing upper-aft radiator and two paired
coil/service housings. Two broad internal belly-tank service bands identify
the two separate Jump-1 fuel loads without external tanks. Three modest stern
field apertures and two power grilles identify the two-gravity C/C plant. One
lower port/ventral scoop grid and two visible/two hidden processor grilles
support independent survey operations.

Civic Survey Works uses warm white `#F4F0D8`, saturated cyan `#25A9B8`, lime
`#A8C83E`, polished chrome/aluminum, and dark teal recesses. White organizes
the hull and observatory; cyan identifies mission shoulders, lab frames,
launch/cargo service, fuel bands, and stern panels; lime identifies probes,
specimen handling, safety, processors, and restrained turret collars; chrome
protects rings, optics, Jump, scoop, and drive edges. Bright public-service
color remains legible and accountable without disguising the armed fit.
