# Humboldt Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 034*

## Mechanical identity

- Family: `family-34`, Humboldt
- Members: `ship-34` and `ship-43`
- Shipyard path: Path 3, Outer Reach Cooperative
- Hull: 200 displacement tons / approximately 2,800 m³
- Configuration: standard; technology level 11
- Shared protection: two points of crystaliron armor
- Shared performance: Jump drive B / Jump-2; maneuver drive B / thrust 2; one
  jump and two weeks of power endurance
- Shared accommodation and field equipment: four staterooms, one emergency low
  berth, a one-bed medical bay, fuel scoop and processor, and mining drones
- Shared armament: one double turret with one beam laser and one sandcaster;
  one additional hardpoint remains closed and empty
- Shared role: self-supporting prospecting and independent belt mining

The records describe the same prospecting hull from two source fits. Ship 34
allocates a 45-ton contained hangar, 31 tons of cargo, basic military
electronics, a Model/3 computer, twenty sand canisters, and a five-person crew.
Ship 43 allocates a 50-ton contained hangar, 27 tons of cargo, basic civilian
electronics, a Model/1bis computer, forty canisters, and a four-person crew.
These are internal partitions, electronics modules, stores, and manning. Both
fits retain one common 50-ton hangar shell and door; ship 34 uses five tons of
its envelope for a cutter workshop and ready room. Exact exterior reuse is
required.

## Canonical family architecture

The Humboldt is 43.0 m long, 17.0 m wide, and 11.0 m high. It is an integrated
but deliberately non-aerodynamic standard hull: a blunt hexagonal forward
pressure house, a deep central work-and-hangar block held in a heavy saddle
frame, and a compact faceted aft engineering drum tied together by one broad
dorsal load spine. Chamfered corners and recessed voids let the envelope
plausibly enclose approximately 2,800 m³ without reading as a streamlined ship.

The forward house carries a five-port bridge brow, exactly four small
stateroom windows in one protected strip, and one recessed circular crew
docking collar. The central port face carries one closed 9.0 × 5.2 m
vertically split mining-cutter hangar shutter inside a deep brushed-aluminum
frame. One smaller closed mining-drone drawer lies immediately forward of the
hangar, while a low diagnostic/low-berth service panel lies aft. The bay shell
is sized for fifty displacement tons in both records; no carried cutter is
included or visible.

One common 3.5 m dorsal turret ring carries a compact mixed double turret. Its
two apertures are visibly different: one narrow dark beam-laser optic and one
blunt four-port sandcaster projector. A second circular hardpoint cover remains
closed and flush on the aft load spine. No canister or other ammunition is
visible.

The internal Jump drive reads through two low longitudinal coil-service drums
partly buried under the port and starboard shoulders, each divided by three
patch-polished collar segments. They are service housings, not luminous rings.
Two explicit rectangular fuel-scoop throats sit beneath the forward house; one
ribbed processor grille and exposed protected conduits cross the work block.
Two large hexagonal aft maneuver apertures, one central power grille, four
recessed landing-pad doors, two field grapples, standardized repair panels, and
heavy door frames complete the chassis.

- one closed aft hardpoint cover with no installed second mount;

  processor grille, protected conduits, aft engineering drum, maneuver
  apertures, power grille, landing-pad doors, grapples, and repair panels; and

missile launcher, identical weapon barrels, external Jump ring, glowing drive
