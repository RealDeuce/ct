# Nansen Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production singleton family 076*

## Mechanical identity

- Record: `ship-76` Nansen
- Path: Civic Survey Works (P4)
- Hull: 500 displacement tons / approximately 7,000 m³; streamlined
- Dimensions: 68.0 m long, 25.0 m wide, and 16.5 m high
- Fit: TL11, four armor points, Jump E / Jump-2 with fuel for two jumps,
  maneuver H, power H, two weeks endurance, and advanced electronics
- Expedition support: ten staterooms, six low berths, two emergency low berths,
  two laboratories, two-bed medical bay, two probes, repair drones, six fuel
  processors, 30 tons cargo, and two separate hangars
- Weapons: two double-beam turrets, one double-sandcaster turret, one double-
  missile turret, and one unused fire-control station; no point defense
- Carried craft: Jason in the 30-ton full hangar; the 14-ton standard hangar is
  empty and unassigned

Nansen is an independent long-range-scout family. It must not reuse Darwin's
survey-lantern crown or Magellan's terraced exploration-caravel. All three share
Civic Survey's instrument, finish, and public-service vocabulary without
sharing pressure-hull geometry.

## Canonical architecture

The hull is a 68.0 × 25.0 × 16.5 m “survey sounder”: a calm broad rounded-
hexagonal pressure body, blunt faceted beak prow, low eight-pane command brow,
two long integral sensor shoulders, deep continuous expedition keel, two-level
port expedition-bay spine, and tapered but substantial thrust-three stern. The
balanced mass is a filled multi-deck spacecraft rather than an aircraft,
watercraft, tank arrangement, or collection of detached pods.

The upper port bay is one closed 8.8 × 5.2 m two-leaf full-hangar shutter with
an approximately 22 m internal axis. It clears the approved 21.0 × 7.6 × 4.1 m
Jason, which remains internal. A heavy structural rail separates it from the
smaller closed 6.0 × 4.2 m two-leaf standard-hangar shutter below. That bay has
at least a 13 m internal axis but no assigned craft. A separate closed 8.0 ×
3.6 m cargo/sample shutter serves the 30-ton hold.

Exactly five of ten stateroom windows appear in one port occupancy strip and
five are hidden starboard. One broad faceted dark-teal laboratory observation
bay occupies the forward port sensor shoulder and a matching bay is hidden
starboard. These are instrument workspaces, not bridges, hangars, or weapon
apertures. One formal airlock, three visible/three hidden low-berth panels, one
visible/one hidden emergency-berth panel, one plain medical hatch, and one
repair-drone slot remain closed and unmarked.

## Hardpoint and instrument map

Five exterior fire-control coordinates express four installed double turrets
and one unused station:

1. Visible forward upper-port shoulder: double beam-laser turret with exactly
   two short optical emitters.
2. Visible lower-mid port keel lip: double-sandcaster turret with exactly two
   blunt multi-port projectors.
3. Visible mid-dorsal coordinate: one closed circular unused hardpoint cover.
4. Hidden upper-aft starboard shoulder: the second double-beam turret.
5. Hidden lower-forward starboard/ventral coordinate: double-missile turret.

The standard plate therefore shows two of four installed weapons plus the
closed fifth coordinate. Hidden weapons do not migrate to the crown merely to
be displayed. There are no point-defense nodes.

Two probe coffers use one visible port and one hidden starboard position.
Circular instrument collars, two ceramic radomes, fine antenna grids, and
optical flats remain visibly scientific and unarmed. Never turn an instrument,
probe coffer, processor, coupling, hangar fitting, door latch, or recovery guide
into a turret or point-defense node.

## Fuel, Jump, drive, and armor

Six processors form a bilateral bank with three round ribbed grilles visible
port and three hidden starboard, plus one manifold and coupling. Nansen has no
recorded scoop.

The internal Jump-E drive reads through two segmented transverse radiator/
service bands around the aft waist and four flush oval coil-service blisters,
two visible and two hidden. Fuel for the second Jump remains internal; it does
not create an external tank. There is no Jump ring, hoop, portal, or glow.
Three broad port maneuver apertures and three hidden starboard apertures flank
two separate power grilles. Two flowing protective bands, reinforced component
frames, and overlapping serviceable plates express four armor points.

## Civic finish and catalog plate

- Warm white `#F4F0D8`: pressure hull, command, expedition keel, and laboratory
  surfaces
- Cyan `#25A9B8`: both hangar shutters and their structural rail, lower datum,
  laboratory frame, cargo shutter, processor surrounds, and selected Jump and
  engineering panels
- Lime `#A8C83E`: radomes, instruments, probe, processor status, recovery, and
  turret collars
- Bright chrome/aluminum: bridge, laboratory, access, hangar, cargo, weapon,
  fuel, Jump, landing, and drive interfaces
- Dark teal/gunmetal: glazing, recesses, shutter seams, slats, and grilles

Use a complete front-port three-quarter view about 12 degrees above with
restrained near-orthographic perspective, 3:2 landscape, warm upper-front-port
key, cool aft rim, charcoal stars, and faint teal grid. Render as original
late-1970s/early-1980s gouache-and-airbrush technical art. No text, logo,
watermark, open door, deployed probe, weapon fire, planet, modern CGI, or
franchise styling.

```text
2-hidden coil covers, 3-visible/3-hidden maneuver apertures, and four-point
armor. Neutral aluminum and ceramic primer, period gouache and airbrush.

Nansen fit: preserve every anchor coordinate. Fit the visible upper-port ring
with exactly two beam optics and the lower-port ring with exactly two blunt
sandcaster projectors. Keep the second beam and missile turrets hidden. Keep

hangar empty, probes stowed, and all instruments unarmed. Apply Civic warm

```

## Production sequence and provenance

Generated for Cepheus Trader on 2026-08-20 with OpenAI image generation in
built-in mode. The neutral pass rendered seven bridge panes; one scoped
correction subdivided the brow to eight while preserving every other boundary.
The anchor's mid-dorsal circular cover is retained as the source-recorded fifth,
unused fire-control coordinate. The final plate derives from that corrected
anchor and uses Magellan only as a Civic finish reference.
