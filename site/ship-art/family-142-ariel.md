# Ariel Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production singleton family 142*

## Mechanical identity

- Member: `ship-142` Ariel
- Native path: Path 1, Concord Exchange Yards
- Role: luxury boat for passenger transfer and overnight excursions
- Hull: 30 displacement tons / approximately 420 m³; streamlined
- Technology level: 11
- Drives: maneuver `sJ`, power `sJ` / thrust 6; no Jump drive
- Protection: one crystaliron layer / four armor points
- Control: two-person control cabin
- Occupancy: eight acceleration seats and two compact staterooms
- Access and cargo: one pressure airlock and 4.2 tons of baggage/cargo
- Endurance: one week
- Armament: none

Ariel is produced before the Stevenson family because records 139-141 carry it
in a 30-ton full hangar. Its adopted 7.8 m beam and 4.8 m height determine that
hangar's minimum clear door dimensions.

## Canonical architecture

Ariel is 19.0 m long, 7.8 m wide, and 4.8 m high. A broad rounded-lozenge
pressure body combines a softly faceted drooping prow, an elevated narrow cabin
roof, thick integral lower shoulder chines, and a tapered oval aft drive cowl.
The occupied fraction of that streamlined envelope plausibly encloses about
420 m³. The silhouette is deliberately distinct from the longer Argonaut
clipper and more utilitarian Wayfarer boat.

Six small control panes form a shallow forward brow. One straight port
occupancy band has exactly six bays: four blue panes in two paired groups for
the seated passenger cabin and two warmer bronze-blue panes for the compact
staterooms. One 2.4 m port pressure airlock lies beneath an oval docking collar.
A separate 2.8 m aft-low baggage door serves the 4.2-ton hold.

The tapered stern has two broad oval maneuver-field apertures around one small
power grille. Four landing-pad doors and two carrier tie-down sockets remain
flush. A coherent chrome-edged crystaliron band follows the leading and lower
edge. The boat has no turret socket, weapon, fuel-processor grille, Jump-drive
landmark, external tank, separate aircraft wing, or exposed landing gear.

- Vermilion `#E44B2D` airlock, baggage-door, and aft-drive accents

- Dark-blue passenger glazing and warmer bronze-blue private panes
