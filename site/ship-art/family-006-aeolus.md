# Aeolus Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 006*

## Mechanical identity

- Family: `family-6`, Aeolus
- Members: `ship-6` Aeolus, `ship-176` Boreas, and `ship-177` Zephyrus
- Hull: 20 displacement tons / approximately 280 m³
- Configuration: streamlined
- Technology level: 11
- Shared drives: `sF` maneuver and `sF` power / thrust 6
- Shared control: one-person cockpit
- Shared access: one pressure airlock
- Shared processing: one small-craft fuel processor
- Shared endurance: one week
- Shared limitations: no Jump drive and no installed weapon

These are three production fits of one fast-launch platform. They require one
identical chassis and three final plates. Cabin glazing, protective finish, and
formal trim may change only inside the family-standard sockets described below.

## Canonical family anchor

The Aeolus is a single-deck, twenty-ton atmospheric launch with an elongated
flattened-lozenge pressure body. Its canonical maximum dimensions are 18.5 m
long, 6.2 m wide, and 4.5 m high. The tapered cockpit, broad cabin body, shallow
lower service keel, and neatly faired aft drive shoulders occupy approximately
54 percent of that envelope, plausibly enclosing the required 280 m³.

The bow is a low faceted one-person cockpit with three protected panes. A long
horizontal cabin rail occupies the port side and contains six equally spaced
standardized aperture sockets; individual fits may glaze or blank these
sockets, but may not move the rail or its framing. A human-scale rectangular
pressure airlock follows the cabin rail. A broad flush cargo door lies low on
the aft port quarter. A narrow, chrome-edged fuel-processing intake and service
grille sits beneath the airlock.

The shared `sF` installation reads as two large recessed circular field
housings on the aft shoulders and a central power-service aperture at the
stern. The dorsal centerline remains smooth except for a low flush sensor oval
and service seams. There is no turret, weapon aperture, missile cell, Jump
ring, wing, tail, or landing skid.

### Invariants

Every member preserves exactly:

- the 18.5 × 6.2 × 4.5 m envelope and single-deck flattened-lozenge body;
- cockpit geometry and glazing;
- six-socket cabin rail position, size, and chrome frame;
- airlock, cargo door, fuel-processing grille, and dorsal sensor positions;
- aft field housings, stern aperture, shell seams, and lower service keel;
- camera, crop, perspective, backdrop, and lighting; and
- the absence of weapons, Jump-drive cues, wings, tail, and landing skids.

## Variant delta: `ship-6` Aeolus

- Role: utility launch
- Protection: no armor
- Accommodation: twelve acceleration seats
- Cargo: 4.4 tons
- Visible cabin fit: glaze all six port aperture sockets as small deep-set
  passenger windows
- Working fit: emphasize the standard cargo door and fuel-processing grille;
  keep the shell light and clean rather than armored
- Livery: warm ivory upper shell, royal-blue lower keel, vermilion airlock and
  cargo-door safety stripe, polished aluminum/chrome rail and drive collars
- Prohibited additions: stateroom-sized windows, armor overlays, weapons,
  external tanks, or utility machinery not present in the record

## Variant delta: `ship-176` Boreas

- Role: captain's gig
- Protection: two points of whole-hull crystaliron armor
- Accommodation: three compact staterooms / up to six passengers
- Cargo: 3.9 tons
- Visible cabin fit: combine the six standard sockets into three paired,
  moderately wider cabin windows without moving the outer rail or mullion grid
- Protective fit: deeper window reveals, protected shell seams, and a
  continuous armor lip around the airlock and drive shoulders
- Livery: warm ivory armor shell, a broad royal-blue cabin band, vermilion
  airlock, and restrained bright chrome around the cabin rail and field housings
- Prohibited additions: command antennae, flag symbols, weapons, or a changed
  cockpit/cargo/drive arrangement

## Variant delta: `ship-177` Zephyrus

- Role: flag barge
- Protection: two points of whole-hull crystaliron armor
- Accommodation: four compact staterooms / four officers or dignitaries
- Cargo: 1.9 tons
- Visible cabin fit: glaze four individual standard sockets as larger private
  cabin windows and blank the remaining two with flush ivory armor panels
- Protective fit: identical armored shell and deep component reveals to Boreas
- Livery: warm ivory armor shell, royal-blue lower keel, a formal vermilion
  longitudinal cabin stripe, and the family’s brightest polished chrome trim
- Prohibited additions: royal insignia, flags, oversized observation glass,
  special command sensors, weapons, or any geometry not fitted by the record

## Concord Exchange finish

- Primary shell: warm ivory `#F1E3C2`
- Service and recognition panels: vermilion `#E44B2D`
- Lower keel and occupied-zone bands: royal blue `#2354A3`
- Trim: polished aluminum and bright chrome
- Condition: prosperous scheduled or official transport, clean and maintained,
  with light atmospheric wear only at the lower leading edge and airlock sill

All three plates must read as products of the same orderly commercial yard.
Their different status comes from window treatment, armor depth, and disciplined
use of the shared palette, not from unrelated silhouettes or invented heraldry.
