# Aviator Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 117*

## Mechanical identity

- Family: `family-117`, Aviator
- Members: `ship-117` Lilienthal, `ship-119` Wright, and `ship-120` Mitchell
- Configuration: standard; technology level 11
- Shared protection: four armor points, radiation shielding, and armored
  bulkheads around bridge, drives, and ordnance magazines
- Shared control: advanced electronics, command bridge with holographic
  controls, capital-core-3 computer, emergency power, and improved magazines
- Shared endurance: one Jump-2 and two weeks of power-plant endurance
- Shared facilities: one full hangar, two flight decks, one recovery deck,
  scoop and fifteen fuel processors
- Shared limitations: large institutional carriers whose combat value and
  logistics are inseparable from their embarked groups

This is a scaled carrier lineage rather than one invariant hull. Every block
retains the same cross-section and module grammar while adding length, modest
beam and height, hangar capacity, weapon stations, and deck clearance.

## Canonical lineage architecture

An Aviator is an integrated axial carrier with a blunt low command prow, one
recessed eight-pane bridge brow, a strong center keel/spine, paired shoulder
flight-deck tunnels, one lower-center recovery tunnel, a deep rectangular
hangar core assembled from repeated modules, and a squared engineering block.
It is a non-aerodynamic standard hull, not a winged wet-navy carrier or a
streamlined cruiser.

The two shoulder launch mouths and the separate recovery mouth dominate the
prow. They are full craft-handling tunnels with bright datum rails, recovery
beacons, armored lips, and approach guides. They are never small generic doors
and never become open atmospheric runways. Large ribbed maintenance/boat
shutters repeat along the visible port hangar wall. The embarked craft remain
inside on all recognition plates.

The blocks grow by adding one hangar module at a time:

- Lilienthal uses three modules and three visible ribbed shutters.
- Wright inserts a fourth module without rescaling the bridge, turret rings,
  access fixtures, or first-block deck mouths.
- Mitchell inserts a fifth module and modestly grows the shared cross-section.
  Only its launch and recovery mouths widen for the larger strike fighter.

Filled-volume ratios remain approximately 29, 29, and 27 percent of the three
bounding boxes. The resulting internal void is deliberate flight-deck, hangar,
handling, and structural volume rather than an implausibly thin pressure shell.

### Embarked-craft accounting and clearances

The full-hangar allocations exactly match their craft:

- Lilienthal: 30 × 10-ton Icarus I + 2 × 20-ton Caduceus + 2 × 30-ton
  Wayfarer Cargo = 400 tons.
- Wright: 50 × 10-ton Icarus II + 2 × 20-ton Caduceus + 2 × 30-ton
  Wayfarer Cargo = 600 tons.
- Mitchell: 50 × 10-ton Icarus II + 15 × 15-ton Bellerophon + 2 × 20-ton
  Caduceus + 2 × 30-ton Wayfarer Cargo = 825 tons.

Lilienthal and Wright's two launch tunnels are approximately 8.0 × 4.2 m and
their recovery tunnel is approximately 9.0 × 4.8 m. They handle the canonical
11.8 × 5.8 × 2.9 m Icarus through longitudinal deck lanes. Mitchell widens its
launch mouths to approximately 9.4 × 4.8 m and recovery mouth to approximately
10.5 × 5.3 m for the 14.8 × 7.2 × 3.4 m Bellerophon. Broad side shutters provide
maintenance and transfer access for the 17.5 × 6.4 × 4.6 m Caduceus and
18.8 × 8.4 × 4.0 m Wayfarer; they are not additional flight decks.

## Weapon station maps

Every turret uses the common 3–4 m ring and a low 1.5–2.5 m housing. Beam
triples use three narrow optical apertures. Mixed triples use two closed
missile-shutter grids and one short blunt multi-port sandcaster aperture; no
missile is exposed. Point-defense lenses remain less than one quarter of a
turret-ring diameter.

The port three-quarter plate exposes approximately half of each battery:

| Block | Full battery | Visible plate | Hidden half | Point defense |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lilienthal | 10 beam + 9 mixed = 19 | 4 beam + 6 mixed = 10 | 6 beam + 3 mixed = 9 | 13 visible + 12 hidden = 25 |
| Wright | 14 beam + 10 mixed = 24 | 5 beam + 7 mixed = 12 | 9 beam + 3 mixed = 12 | 15 visible + 15 hidden = 30 |
| Mitchell | 14 beam + 12 mixed = 26 | 6 beam + 7 mixed = 13 | 8 beam + 5 mixed = 13 | 17 visible + 16 hidden = 33 |

The modest weapon-type asymmetry concentrates mixed defensive mounts around the
visible flight-deck shoulders while the hidden arcs carry more beam mounts. It
does not change the exact full-ship totals. Never add the hidden stations to the
standard plate merely to display the entire catalog battery.

## Drive, fuel, and engineering landmarks

Lilienthal and Wright share one segmented non-glowing Jump radiator and two
flush coil-service housings for the Jump-T drive. Mitchell keeps those family
locations, enlarges the radiator, and uses three coil-service housings for its
Jump-Y fit. These are maintenance and heat-management landmarks, not a luminous
ring or exposed drive core.

All three have one ventral scoop grid and fifteen processor-service grilles.
Four recessed maneuver-field apertures and two ribbed power grilles occupy the
squared stern. Mitchell may enlarge the Z/Z apertures within those inherited
stations; it may not replace the engineering block or sprout engine nacelles.

## Shipyard finishes

### Lilienthal and Wright — Admiralty Line Works

- Deep navy `#203B69` main hull and keel
- Warm white `#E8E5D4` command brow, flight shoulders, and access blocks
- Signal red `#C43C35` deck lips, recovery marks, and weapon shutters
- Bright aluminum turret rings, rails, radiator surrounds, collars, and datum
  lines
- Formal repeated color blocks expressing mass-produced fleet authority

Wright repeats Lilienthal's blocks across its added module. It does not acquire
a new livery or an independently redesigned superstructure.

### Mitchell — Tempest Arsenal

- Ultramarine `#3046A6` main hull
- Violet `#6B3F91` command spine and engineering shoulders
- Warning yellow `#F2C84B` deck lips, mixed-turret shutters, recovery marks,
  and hangar service accents
- Bright chrome rails, rings, radiator surrounds, collars, and sharp datum lines
- Controlled aft heat staining and saturated opaque color, never neon

Tempest treatment follows the exact Aviator block boundaries. The path change
does not erase the inherited bridge, cross-section, deck arrangement, module
rhythm, or engineering coordinates.

## Family invariants

Every block preserves:

- the blunt eight-pane command prow and recognizable carrier cross-section;
- two shoulder launch tunnels and one lower-center recovery tunnel;
- the center spine, repeated hangar-module grammar, and squared stern;
- turret-ring scale, mixed/beam recognition, tiny PD scale, scoop region,
  processor banks, drive-station pattern, landing doors, recovery beacons,
  datum rails, and major seam logic;
- complete front-port three-quarter camera, crop, backdrop, and lighting; and
- the absence of exposed craft, visible missiles, atmospheric runways, wings,
  tails, masts, weapon bays, barbettes, and glowing Jump structures.

Do not achieve later-block size merely by scaling the earlier image. Add the
specified repeated module, extend the decks and spine, and keep human-scale
fixtures stable. Do not show more than the allocated visible half-battery.

## Catalog plate

Use a complete front-port three-quarter view from about 12 degrees above
centerline, restrained near-orthographic perspective, 3:2 landscape, warm
upper-front-port key, weak cool aft rim, charcoal sparse starfield, and faint
teal plotting grid. Render original late-1970s/early-1980s gouache-and-airbrush
technical art with saturated opaque paint and bright metal. No text, labels,
logo, watermark, other craft, weapon fire, planet, motion blur, modern gray
rendering, or recognizable franchise design.
