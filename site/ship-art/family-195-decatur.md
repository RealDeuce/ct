# Decatur Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 195*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/member: `family-195`; `ship-195` Decatur
- Hull: 300 displacement tons / approximately 4,200 m³
- Dimensions: 60 m long, 19 m wide, and 14.5 m high
- Configuration: standard; technology level 11; standard design
- Protection: eight armor points from two crystaliron layers; stealth treatment
- Drives: Jump-C / Jump-2, one Jump, maneuver J, and power J / thrust 6
- Endurance: four weeks of power-plant fuel
- Control: advanced electronics and fib Model/3 computer
- Personnel: 18 crew including ten marines; nine staterooms, armory, four
  detention cells, and five emergency low berths
- Mission capacity: 17 tons cargo; no external load or hangar
- Battery: two triple-missile turrets and one triple-beam turret
- Ammunition: 120 smart missiles
- Fuel handling: one scoop and five processors

## Independent Admiralty corvette

Decatur is a tightly packed 60 × 19 × 14.5 m standard-hull corvette. A long
faceted command prow, repeated armored frames, protected boarding waist, deep
magazine shoulders, strong centerline, and dominant aft engineering battery
express regular fleet construction. Exactly six narrow bridge panes sit behind
the armored brow. Flush advanced-sensor cheeks and recessed seams support the
stealth treatment without turning the ship into a featureless black wedge.

The marine waist has one closed formal pressure-airlock collar with handhold
sockets, an armory shutter, four detention-service panels, and emergency access.
A restrained five-window armored crew group indexes the nine staterooms rather
than serving as a literal deck plan. The small 17-ton cargo shutter remains
closed. There is no boat bay or external craft.

## Turret map and fields of fire

Three fixed hardpoint coordinates cover complementary approaches:

- the visible forward port/upper-shoulder ring carries one triple-beam cup;
- the visible outward-canted aft port/lower-flank ring carries one closed
  triple-missile cup; and
- the hidden ventral/starboard ring carries the second triple-missile cup.

The beam cup has exactly three short narrow optical emitters in one low faceted
housing. The visible missile cup has exactly three armored rack shutters or
gridded faces and no exposed missile. The hidden missile station covers lower,
starboard, and stern approaches while the visible pair cover forward, dorsal,
port, aft, and near-ventral arcs. Never migrate all three mounts to the crown or
show the hidden turret simply to display the complete inventory.

Three hardpoints are below the large-battery distribution threshold, but the
forward-shoulder/lower-flank/ventral-starboard map still prevents a dorsal-only
assumption. Sensors, airlock hardware, magazine seams, processors, and stern
apertures remain barrel-free.

## Drives, fuel, and armor recognition

Jump-C uses one compact segmented non-glowing radiator and paired field-coil
service housings. The J/J six-gravity stern is the dominant machinery mass:
exactly six huge recessed maneuver apertures and four power grilles are far
larger than merchant fittings on an equal hull. One protected scoop field lies
on the lower forward keel and five processor banks occupy separate armored
service positions.

Eight-point armor appears as overlapping facets, protected leading edges,
short load paths, recessed machinery, and armored magazine boundaries. Stealth
treatment uses aligned seams, subdued facet transitions, flush sensors, and low
weapon housings; it does not remove the formal Admiralty color scheme.

## Admiralty finish

Admiralty Line Works uses deep navy `#203B69`, warm white `#E8E5D4`, signal
red `#C43C35`, and bright aluminum. Navy dominates armor and engineering;
white identifies the command brow and disciplined panels; red marks boarding,
weapon, magazine, scoop, and drive safety boundaries; bright metal forms datum
lines, collars, handholds, radiator, and leading edges. The saturated period
finish communicates mass-produced fleet authority rather than camouflage.
