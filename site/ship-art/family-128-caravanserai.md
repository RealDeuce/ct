# Caravanserai Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 128*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/members: `family-128`; `ship-128` Samarkand, `ship-129` Bukhara,
  and `ship-130` Merv
- Hull: 600 displacement tons / approximately 8,400 m³
- Dimensions: 72 m long, 24 m wide, and 15.5 m high
- Configuration: standard; technology level 11
- Shared protection: four points of crystaliron armor
- Shared drives: Jump-H / Jump-2, maneuver J and power J / thrust 3
- Shared endurance: one Jump and two weeks of power-plant fuel
- Shared working systems: scoop, two fuel processors, repair drones, workshop,
  holographic bridge controls, and medical bed
- Shared defense: six one-ton turret stations, six laser point-defense nodes,
  improved magazine, and 20 sandcaster canisters

Samarkand is the Path 2 commercial baseline with basic civilian electronics,
eight middle passengers, 248 tons cargo, five triple beam-laser turrets, and one
single sandcaster. Bukhara is the Path 7 replenishment conversion with basic
military electronics, a twenty-person crew, underway-transfer machinery, and
244 tons cargo; its weapons are unchanged. Merv is the Path 7 assault fit with
sixty-four marines, a 70-ton full vehicle hangar, five tons cargo, three triple
beam turrets, two single particle-beam turrets, and one single sandcaster.

## Shared canonical chassis

The family occupies a 72 × 24 × 15.5 m envelope. Its recognizable shape is a
clipped, forward-driving wedge wrapped around a rectangular pressure-body core,
with protected flank shoulders, a strong lower keel, and a compact high-output
engineering block. Visible steps and joints keep it a standard space hull rather
than an atmospheric airliner. It is distinct from the Cook patrol chassis.

Exactly six protected bridge panes form the recessed forward brow. One pressure
airlock precedes the long port mission waist. Above it, eight fixed occupied-
deck positions serve passenger, crew, or barracks spaces according to fit.

The mission waist is a three-cell conversion frame. Its outer perimeter, three
vertical structural stations, module depth, and leaf centers never move:

- Samarkand uses three broad closed ribbed freight shutters.
- Bukhara retains all three shutters and adds only a compact replenishment
  station in the narrow service zone between the airlock and forward cell.
- Merv replaces the forward cell with an armored muster/service insert and
  combines the middle and aft cells as one full-hangar opening. The retained
  central frame becomes the reinforced mullion between its closed shutter leaves.

The common aft block carries four recessed maneuver-field apertures and two
power grilles. One segmented non-glowing Jump-service radiator, two paired
field-coil service housings, a protected keel scoop, and two processor grilles
remain fixed on all three ships.

## Shared weapon map

Six one-ton turret rings occupy fixed family coordinates. The standard port
three-quarter view exposes exactly three: forward dorsal, lower port shoulder,
and aft dorsal. Three corresponding far/ventral rings are hidden.

- Samarkand and Bukhara show triple-beam cups in both dorsal rings and the
  single sandcaster cup on the lower shoulder. Their three hidden rings are
  triple-beam mounts, giving five triple-beam and one sandcaster total.
- Merv retains the forward triple-beam and lower sandcaster, but changes the
  visible aft cup to a heavier single particle-beam. Of its three hidden rings,
  two are triple-beam and one is particle-beam, giving the recorded 3/2/1 fit.

Six 0.4–0.8 m point-defense laser nodes are also fixed. Exactly three tiny
blue-white lenses are readable in the catalog view and three are occluded.
They remain far smaller than a turret ring.

## Variant recognition

### Samarkand — fast trader

All three conversion cells are closed cargo shutters, making the 248-ton hold
the dominant flank feature. All eight occupied positions are dark-bronze
passenger/crew glazing. Flush commercial sensor strips occupy the common prow
cheeks. Venture Passage Works finish uses sunflower `#F2C230`, cobalt
`#174A8B`, signal orange `#E96D2F`, and protected bright chrome rails and
collars. The character is fast, expensive frontier commerce, not naval service.

### Bukhara — fleet replenishment ship

Three freight shutters and their frames remain unchanged. The compact transfer
station contains a short folded boom, two protected hose drums, dry-break fluid
couplings, stores-transfer socket, cable guides, and replaceable wear rails.
It consumes only the four-ton difference from Samarkand and must not displace a
whole freight cell or resemble a weapon, clamp, or second scoop. Compact
military sensor blisters replace commercial strips inside the same cheek sockets.

Bukhara changes to Admiralty Line Works deep navy `#203B69`, warm white
`#E8E5D4`, signal red `#C43C35`, and bright aluminum datum lines. Wear is
concentrated at the transfer station and cargo sills.

### Merv — assault transport

The Bukhara transfer station is removed and its service panel restored. A broad
approximately 18 × 5.5 m closed full-hangar shutter spans the middle and aft
cells, with the shared intervening frame retained as a reinforced load-bearing
mullion. A low vehicle sill, armored ribbing, maintenance sockets, and generous
internal axis make the 70-ton vehicle package credible. No vehicle appears
outside. The forward cell contains two closed troop airlocks, powered-armor
service sockets, handholds, and one small stores hatch. Its outer frame remains
unchanged. The eight upper positions receive deeper armored brows.

Merv retains the Bukhara Admiralty palette, sensors, drives, and armor. Red
safety identification concentrates around the hangar and troop-access insert.

## Invariants

Preserve the dimensions, clipped-wedge/rectangular-core silhouette, six bridge
panes, three-cell mission-waist outer frame and vertical stations, eight upper
positions, airlock coordinate, six turret centers, six point-defense centers,
sensor-cheek sockets, armor bands, Jump radiator and field housings, scoop,
processor grilles, maneuver apertures, power grilles, landing geometry,
principal seams, camera, crop, backdrop, and lighting.

Never add an atmospheric fairing, wing, fin, exterior tank, carried craft,
docking clamp, exposed vehicle, open shutter, giant weapon, extra turret or PD
node, glowing Jump ring, or flame. A military conversion changes mission-cell
contents and finish; it does not become a new hull.
