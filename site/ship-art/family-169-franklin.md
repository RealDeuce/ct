# Franklin Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 169*

## Mechanical identity

- Family/members: `family-169`; `ship-169` Franklin I, `ship-170` Poor
  Richard, `ship-171` Franklin II, `ship-172` Pennsylvania, `ship-173`
  Deborah, `ship-174` Postmaster, and `ship-175` Gulf Stream
- Hull: 100 displacement tons / approximately 1,400 m³
- Dimensions: 38 m long, 14 m wide, and 8.5 m high
- Configuration: streamlined; technology level 12
- Shared protection: four armor points from one crystaliron layer
- Shared Jump drive: Jump-A / Jump-2
- Shared services: holographic controls, bis Model/2 computer, fuel processor,
  autodoc, emergency low berth, and two weeks of power-plant fuel
- Shared limitations: no fuel scoop and no installed weapon
- Reserved armament: one unused hardpoint and fire-control station; no PD nodes

Franklin I, Franklin II, and Pennsylvania use one Jump-2 load, B/B drives, basic
civilian electronics, Concord finish, no external mission equipment, and the
same closed commercial waist. Their accommodation and cargo allocations are
internal, so they are exterior-equivalent and share one production plate.

Poor Richard uses A/A drives and adds a four-ton vehicle hangar. Deborah is a
B/B passenger trader. Postmaster uses C/C machinery for six-gravity dispatch
work. Gulf Stream uses B/B, advanced sensors, and two complete Jump-2 fuel
loads.

## Shared canonical chassis

The family occupies a 38 × 14 × 8.5 m compact two-deck pressure shell. A
rounded clipped teardrop prow, modest command brow, flat-sided conversion waist,
strong lower keel, and short faired engineering tail distinguish the design
from both a 20-ton launch and the longer 300-ton Banks line. Exactly four dark
panes protect the bridge.

One long approximately 16 × 4.2 m port conversion frame lies between a
forward personnel airlock and aft service door. Its outer perimeter, lower sill,
depth, attachment datum, and relation to the doors are immutable. The neutral
anchor's three primer tooling panels are not structural modules; production
fits may combine them as two broad revenue bays while preserving the outer
frame. Passenger rails, freight doors, vehicle leaves, priority hatch, and fuel
service panels all remain within that boundary.

Landing-gear doors remain flush and closed. Paired cheek sockets accept civilian
or advanced sensors without moving. One compact upper-aft Jump radiator, two
low field-coil/service housings, a processor grille, refuelling coupling, four
maneuver sockets, and two power-grille sockets remain common coordinates.

### Franklin I, Franklin II, and Pennsylvania

Two chrome-collared ribbed freight shutters dominate the waist. The small
forward cheek strip is a commercial sensor strip, not passenger glazing. The
shared plate is honest for all three because stateroom density and the
19.5-versus-27.5-ton cargo allocation do not move a door, drive, sensor, mount,
or other exterior component.

### Poor Richard

The forward bay stays a freight shutter. The aft bay becomes a closed two-leaf
vehicle door with a low sill, protected rails, accessible hinges, and brick-red
hazard blocks. No vehicle is shown. Shallower throats inside the common stern
sockets distinguish A/A two-gravity machinery.

### Deborah

The forward bay contains exactly six human-scale dark-teal passenger windows
in one protected chrome rail. A smaller aft freight shutter preserves the
7.5-ton light-cargo role. The windows index occupied service rather than map one
pane to every room or passenger.

### Postmaster

The forward bay contains exactly four passenger windows. The aft bay carries a
smaller closed high-security priority-cargo hatch with locking dogs and an
orange seal line. Deeper throats inside the same stern sockets identify C/C
six-gravity machinery; the hull outline does not grow.

### Gulf Stream

Both bays become flush reinforced internal-fuel/service panels with datum ribs,
protected caps, and inspection edges. They do not bulge beyond the hull and are
not external tanks. A tiny stores hatch accounts for the 1.5-ton hold. Advanced
cheek optics, two modest circular instrument collars, and a ceramic sensor
detail occupy existing sockets and remain barrel-free.

## Empty hardpoint and coverage

Every member leaves its one forward dorsal/port-shoulder hardpoint unused. The
plate must show the same smooth flush removable armored disk, never an empty
open well or decorative turret. Each record has one unused fire-control station
and no ammunition. No family member has a point-defense node.

Because no weapon is installed, field-of-fire optimization does not justify
inventing a flank, ventral, or aft mount. A later armed conversion would need a
separate approved fit and a coverage audit; the production family stays unarmed.

## Drives and fuel

Jump-A uses one compact non-glowing segmented radiator and two paired low
coil/service housings. Franklin I/II, Pennsylvania, Deborah, and Gulf Stream use
moderate B/B apertures. Poor Richard reduces throat depth for A/A; Postmaster
increases it for C/C. Every change stays inside four fixed maneuver apertures
and two fixed power grilles.

All ships have one processor grille and one protected refuelling coupling but
no scoop. Gulf Stream's second Jump-2 load is internal and appears only as
service panels and datum features, never an external tank or enlarged hull.

## Shipyard finishes

### Concord Exchange Yards

Franklin I/II, Pennsylvania, and Deborah use warm ivory `#F1E3C2`, royal blue
`#2354A3`, vermilion `#E44B2D`, and polished aluminum/chrome. Long clean bands,
bright rectangular collars, and orderly access communicate maintained small-
ship commerce and charter service.

### Outer Reach Cooperative

Poor Richard and Gulf Stream use avocado `#7A9238`, ochre `#C9902C`, brick
red `#A63F2F`, and brushed or patch-polished aluminum. Accessible machinery and
replaceable panels read as rugged frontier practicality, still using clear
period color.

### Venture Passage Works

Postmaster uses sunflower `#F2C230`, cobalt `#174A8B`, signal orange
`#E96D2F`, and protected bright chrome. The forward-driving high-thrust finish
marks expensive priority movement without turning the dispatch ship naval gray.

## Catalog plate and reusable prompts

Use a complete front-port three-quarter view about 12 degrees above centerline,
restrained near-orthographic perspective, 3:2 landscape, warm upper-front-port
key, weak cool aft rim, charcoal sparse starfield, and faint teal plotting grid.
Render original late-1970s/early-1980s gouache-and-airbrush technical art with
bright opaque enamel and protected metal. No planet, motion blur, modern gray
rendering, or recognizable franchise design.

```text
Neutral anchor: create the unpainted 38 × 14 × 8.5 m, 100-ton Franklin
streamlined two-deck hull with rounded clipped prow, four-pane bridge, fixed
16 × 4.2 m conversion-frame perimeter, forward airlock, aft service door,
one flush closed dorsal hardpoint disk, no PD nodes, compact Jump radiator, two
coil housings, one processor and refuelling coupling, four maneuver sockets,
and two power grilles. No weapon, scoop, open door, paint, text, or glow.

Franklin commerce fit: install two broad closed freight shutters in the frame,
retain the closed hardpoint and B/B sockets, and apply Concord ivory, blue,
vermilion, and chrome. Reuse exactly for ships 169, 171, and 172.

Poor Richard fit: edit Franklin. Keep one freight shutter, replace the other
with a low-sill four-ton vehicle door, reduce drive depth to A/A, keep the
hardpoint closed, and apply Outer Reach avocado, ochre, brick, and aluminum.

Deborah fit: edit Franklin. Replace the forward bay with exactly six passenger
windows and retain a smaller aft cargo shutter. Preserve B/B, the closed
hardpoint, and Concord finish.

Postmaster fit: edit Deborah. Reduce the rail to four windows, replace cargo
with a secure priority hatch, deepen only the fixed stern sockets to C/C, keep
the hardpoint closed, and apply Venture sunflower, cobalt, orange, and chrome.

Gulf Stream fit: edit Poor Richard. Replace both bays with flush internal-fuel
service panels and a tiny stores hatch, restore B/B, add advanced barrel-free
sensors in reserved sockets, keep the hardpoint closed, and retain Outer Reach
finish. No external tank or scoop.
```
