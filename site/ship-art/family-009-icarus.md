# Icarus Family Visual Manifest

*Status: catalog production family 009*

## Mechanical identity

- Family: `family-9`, Icarus
- Members: `ship-9` Icarus I and `ship-11` Icarus II
- Hull: 10 displacement tons / approximately 140 m³
- Configuration: streamlined
- Technology level: 11
- Shared drives: `sC` maneuver and `sC` power / thrust 6
- Shared control: one-person cockpit
- Shared electronics: basic military, model-2 computer with fiber-optic backup
- Shared software: Evade/1 and Fire Control/1
- Shared weapon: one single turret carrying one missile rack
- Shared magazine: twelve standard missiles
- Shared endurance: one week
- Shared limitations: no Jump drive, no airlock, and negligible cargo

These are successive armor blocks of one compact carrier-fighter platform. The
weapon, drive, cockpit, magazine, recovery, and hull geometry are invariant.
Icarus II adds one visible protective layer without becoming a new fighter.

## Canonical family anchor

The Icarus is a wingless single-seat lifting-body interceptor with maximum
dimensions 11.8 m long, 5.8 m wide, and 2.9 m high. Its broad shallow pressure
body, tapered nose, clipped shoulder chines, cockpit blister, and compact aft
drive wedge occupy approximately 71 percent of that bounding envelope,
plausibly enclosing the required approximately 140 m³.

The pilot sits forward beneath a low three-pane armored cockpit canopy. The
canopy is an access hatch, not a pressure airlock; there is no side personnel
door. A circular standardized hardpoint ring lies on the dorsal centerline
immediately behind the cockpit. Both production blocks carry one compact,
shuttered missile-rack turret on this ring. Its external housing is a short
rectangular drum with closed cell shutters and no exposed missiles or gun
barrels. The twelve-round magazine is internal beneath the ring.

The aft third contains two recessed circular field housings and one central
ribbed power-service grille. Bright datum rails on the shoulders lead into
flush carrier-recovery sockets on the ventral chines. Small military sensor
cheeks flank the nose. There are no passenger windows, cargo doors, wings,
tailplanes, external missiles, or landing skids.

### Invariants

Both production blocks preserve exactly:

- the 11.8 × 5.8 × 2.9 m wingless lifting-body silhouette;
- cockpit position, three-pane canopy, nose sensor cheeks, and canopy seam;
- dorsal hardpoint ring, shuttered single missile-rack turret, and magazine bulge;
- twin aft field housings, central power grille, recovery sockets, and datum rails;
- camera, crop, perspective, backdrop, and lighting; and
- the absence of airlock doors, passenger windows, exposed ordnance, gun
  barrels, Jump hardware, wings, tailplanes, and landing skids.

  recessed but relatively light sensor/drive surrounds

  upper cockpit and shoulder panels, signal red `#C43C35` turret shutter and
  recovery marks, bright aluminum datum rails and component collars
- Condition: maintained first-block fleet fighter with restrained carrier wear

## Variant delta: `ship-11` Icarus II

- Protection: two points of whole-hull crystaliron armor
- Cargo: 0.35 tons of residual stores
- Armor reading: preserve the exact hull outline while adding a second nested
  protective band around the cockpit, turret ring, nose sensors, and aft drive
  apertures; deepen component recesses and overlap existing shoulder seams
- Livery: identical Admiralty palette and formal block layout; use the second
  warm-white shoulder inset and doubled bright datum line as recognition cues
- Prohibited: enlarged turret, extra missile cells, second mount, changed
  cockpit, altered drive block, larger hull, or new aerodynamic surface
